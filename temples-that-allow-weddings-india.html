Which temples in India actually allow weddings?

The temples most people search for are mostly the ones that do not host weddings. The ones that do have turned the wedding into a seva with a counter, a slot and a rulebook. Here is the map, grouped by how reliably a planner can deliver a wedding there.

We researched temple notices, Devaswom and endowment portals, court orders and newspaper reports across every state to answer one question for our own families: where can a traditional Hindu wedding actually be held, and under whose permission? The short answer is that trust-run living temples host weddings; protected monuments do not. The Archaeological Survey of India's guidelines, as stated by the Culture Minister in Parliament in March 2023, allow only government bodies to apply for events at centrally protected monuments and expressly prohibit "commercial or religious activities" there (PTI).

Tier 2: established practice, but every rupee figure is from a blog

Weddings are routine at these temples, but the fees and rules circulating online come from pilgrimage blogs that contradict each other. We confirm current terms with the temple office before quoting: Madurai Meenakshi (two or three weddings a day in a designated sannathi, not before the main deity), Palani, Tiruttani, Srirangam, Swamimalai, Thirukkadaiyur in Tamil Nadu; Dwaraka Tirumala, Annavaram, Srisailam in Andhra; Yadadri and Bhadrachalam halls in Telangana (hilltop weddings are not allowed at Yadadri; halls at the base are booked at the office); Attukal in Thiruvananthapuram; Goa's other GSB temples; and ISKCON Dwarka and Juhu.

Tier 3: on request only, after a written yes

These are the temples we are asked about most, and the ones we sell most carefully. Sri Virupaksha Temple, Hampi is a living temple inside a World Heritage area: the endowment office says weddings need archaeology-department clearance, the ASI circle says approving weddings is not its job, and what survives is a stripped ritual with no feast, no cooking, no photography and no large gathering on the premises (Hospet Online, April 2026). Bhoga Nandeeshwara, Nandi Hills hosts weddings in its carved Vasantha Mantapa inside an ASI-protected complex, flowers and fabric only, nothing fixed to stone, with availability that shifts with conservation work. Read our dedicated pages on Hampi and Nandi Hills before you set your heart on either. Also in this group: Somnath (a trust hall exists; marriages are not mentioned), Chhatarpur Delhi (only when organised with the temple), Chidambaram (Pandiyanayakar Sannathi only), Tiruvannamalai (case by case).

Tier 4: darshan only

No devotee weddings are performed at Tirumala's Srivari temple itself, Puri Jagannath, Lingaraj, Kashi Vishwanath, Ayodhya Ram Mandir, Banke Bihari, Mahakaleshwar, Siddhivinayak, Shirdi, Vaishno Devi, Pushkar Brahma, or at the ASI monuments of Konark, Belur, Halebidu, Somnathpur, Ramappa and Thanjavur. We plan these as a pre- or post-wedding blessing visit, not as the venue. Some temples have withdrawn: Bengaluru's Halasuru Someshwara, which once did 100 to 150 weddings a year, stopped after its priests were repeatedly summoned in divorce cases (Hinduism Today).

Rules that apply almost everywhere

  • Hindus only. Tamil Nadu's HR&CE minister limits temple weddings to Hindu Indian nationals; Guruvayur requires a Hindu declaration; TTD, Dharmasthala and the AP and Telangana temples say the same.
  • Bride 18, groom 21, parents present. Most counters require Aadhaar for the couple and both sets of parents, and treat the wedding as an arranged first marriage.
  • Dhoti and saree at the ceremony; non-Hindu guests cannot enter Guruvayur, Puri or Padmanabhaswamy at all, and Tamil Nadu temples require a written undertaking beyond the kodimaram.
  • No food, alcohol or DJ inside. Feasts happen in halls or hotels; photography is restricted or ticketed.
  • The temple receipt is not a marriage certificate. Registration under the Hindu Marriage Act happens separately. See how to get married in a temple.
Our policy: we list a temple as "bookable" only where we have booked it or hold the temple's written terms. Everything else is sold as "on request" after a written yes from the office, never on the strength of a blog.

Questions families ask

Can we get married inside Tirumala temple?

No. Private weddings are not performed inside the Srivari temple. TTD runs a free wedding venue, the Kalyana Vedika, on the hill, and paid weddings happen in TTD mandapams and through the Purohit Sangham. Darshan is arranged around the muhurta.

Can non-Hindus marry in a Hindu temple in India?

At almost every temple we researched, no. Tamil Nadu HR&CE temples, Guruvayur, TTD, Dharmasthala and most others require both parties to be Hindu, and Guruvayur has asked converts for an Arya Samaj certificate. ISKCON venues and Arya Samaj mandirs are the usual route for a Vedic ceremony where one partner is not Hindu by birth; we cover this in our NRI and overseas guide.

Which temple weddings can be booked online?

TTD's Kalyana Vedika and its network of kalyana mandapams, Guruvayur's marriage booking, and the Travancore Devaswom Board's online "marriage" vazhipadu at its temples. Tamil Nadu HR&CE temples and Karnataka Muzrai temples book in person at the temple office.

Is a wedding at an ASI monument ever possible?

Not for a private family. ASI guidelines restrict events to government bodies and classical cultural programmes and prohibit religious activity. Living temples inside ASI zones, such as Virupaksha at Hampi, can still perform a bare ritual under the temple's own administration, with ASI conditions on cooking, photography and gatherings.
