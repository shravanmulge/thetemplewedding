# Temples outside South India that host weddings (2024–2026 practice)

Scope: Maharashtra, Goa, Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, UP, Uttarakhand, Delhi, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal, J&K, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Assam/North-East. Karnataka, TN, Kerala, AP, Telangana excluded.

Legend used throughout: **(a)** = wedding ceremony inside the temple complex itself; **(b)** = temple-trust-run hall/mandap adjacent to or within the campus; **(c)** = only private halls/ashrams/hotels nearby, temple is for darshan/blessing only; **NO** = weddings explicitly not permitted / no evidence of any practice.

Reliability note for the writer: outside Uttarakhand, Goa and a few Delhi/Mumbai temples, almost nothing is published by the temple trusts themselves. A large share of search hits are SEO "marriage procedure" pages (gokshetra.com, prayanamam.com, gotirupati.com) that openly state they are unaffiliated and often unverified; I have cited them only where they are the sole source and flagged them. Wedding-portal listings (WedMeGood, WeddingWire, The Wedding Company) are secondary leads only.

## Key question 0 — Cross-cutting national rules (ASI monuments, legal registration, 2024–26 bans/openings)

### Takeaway
Centrally protected (ASI) monuments cannot host receptions/parties/entertainment without written Central Government permission under Rule 7 of the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Rules 1959, and ASI's stated norm is no new religious ceremonies at protected sites unless they were functioning places of worship when taken over — so ASI-held sites such as Konark and Martand are effectively closed to weddings. The 2024–26 news cycle is dominated by (i) Uttarakhand's state-backed "Wed in India/temple wedding" push (Triyuginarayan, Ukhimath), (ii) temples tightening or litigating wedding access (Bengaluru Halasuru ban, Guwahati refusals, Shimla Ram Mandir nikah row), and (iii) Madras HC asking for an SOP for temple weddings (TN, but indicative).

### Cited Findings
- Rule 7 of the AMASR Rules 1959 "prohibits holding of meetings, reception parties, conferences or entertainment except under and in accordance with a permission in writing granted by the Central Government"; a parliamentary answer notes that "recognized religious functions consisting of congregations, receptions, parties, conferences or entertainment of the religious nature" are exempt at sites where such functions are recognised — [eparlib.sansad.in](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/536201/1/36398.pdf); [vLex copy of 1959 Rules](https://vlex.in/vid/ancient-monuments-and-archaeological-545678310); official text PDF at [ASI Chennai Circle](https://www.asichennai.gov.in/downloads/amasr_rules_1959.pdf) (PDF returned 503 when I tried to fetch it; rule number and gist confirmed via the other two sources).
- Rule 7 was substituted by the 2008 Amendment Rules (G.S.R. 37(E), 15.01.2008); breach of rules 6/7/8 carries a fine (vLex says up to Rs 500 under the Rules; ASI has separately cited imprisonment up to two years and Rs 1 lakh fine under Section 30 of the 1958 Act) — [vLex](https://vlex.in/vid/ancient-monuments-and-archaeological-545678310); [NewsX on Martand Sun Temple](https://www.newsx.com/national/asi-raises-objection-over-jk-l-g-manoj-sinhas-participation-in-puja-at-martand-sun-temple/).
- ASI's stated norm: "no religious prayers are held at ASI-protected sites unless it is an operational place of worship"; prayers allowed only at protected sites that were functioning places of worship when ASI took charge — [Tribune (Martand Sun Temple, J&K)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/asi-fuming-as-lt-governor-manoj-sinha-holds-prayers-at-martand-sun-temple-393277/amp); [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/asi-stance-on-religious-practices-at-monuments/print_manually).
- Protected monuments may be open only sunrise to sunset under the 1959 Rules — [Scribd copy of govt rules book](https://www.scribd.com/document/404263531/GOVT-RULES-BOOK-pdf).
- Konark (UNESCO, ASI): Orissa High Court (Jan 2025) upheld that only ASI-licensed photographers/guides may operate inside; in Nov 2025 ASI barred visitor entry to the Nata Mandap — [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/guides-photographers-at-konark-unesco-site-must-have-asi-licence-hc-1503386366.html); [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/11/09/cal33-od-konark-sun-temple.html). No source found describing any wedding ever permitted at Konark.
- A Dec 2025 debate on "heritage for hire" in Delhi (letting monuments host events) shows the policy is still contested, not settled — [The Star/AFP](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/12/14/heritage-for-hire-in-delhi-the-idea-stirs-a-monumental-debate).
- Legal status: a temple ceremony is not a legal registration. Uttarakhand (Compulsory Registration of Marriage Act 2010, online portal) and Rajasthan (Compulsory Registration of Marriages Act 2009) require registration regardless of venue — [Uttarakhand HC copy of Act](https://highcourtofuttarakhand.gov.in/files/Compulsory_Registration_of_Marriage_Act_2010.pdf); [Uttarakhand e-marriage portal](https://emarriage.eregistrationukgov.in); [Rajasthan Act text](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22441/1/rajasthan_compulsory_registration_of_marriages_act_in_english.pdf).
- 2024–26 news — tightening: Halasuru Someshwara temple, Bengaluru (Karnataka, outside scope but widely reported) confirmed in Dec 2025 that it stopped weddings 6–7 years ago because priests were repeatedly summoned in divorce cases and eloping couples used fake documents; it previously did 100–150 weddings/year — [Hinduism Today/Mathrubhumi](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/2025/12/25/no-more-weddings-here-bengaluru-temple-bans-marriages-over-divorce-troubles/).
- 2024–26 news — Guwahati: In Feb 2025 a man was refused a booked wedding at Basistha temple (and reported similar refusals at Kamakhya and Sukreshwar) because of inter-caste marriage, despite producing a court-marriage certificate — [NE Now, 14 Feb 2025](https://m.nenow.in/article/assam/assam-guwahati-couple-denied-temple-wedding-due-to-inter-caste-marriage/410540); a Kamakhya Devalaya committee source earlier told Guwahati Plus that inter-religion and inter-caste marriages are not permitted there — [Guwahati Plus](https://m.guwahatiplus.com/article/city-temples-refuse-to-perform-inter-religious-marriage-ceremonies/13752) (page now 404; quote from search snippet).
- 2024–26 news — Shimla: April 2026 row over a Muslim family's nikah booked in the community hall of Ram Mandir, Shimla (run by Sood Sabha). President Rajiv Sood said such ceremonies had been held at the hall before, meat/fish/alcohol are banned on premises, and the hall is in the grounds not the temple; the committee then ruled that only the reception could be held there, with a written guarantee of no religious activity, extra CCTV, and power cut/police if violated — [IANS, 7 Apr 2026](https://ianslive.in/row-erupts-in-shimla-over-proposed-nikah-at-ram-mandir-hall--20260407152150); [Devdiscourse, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/entertainment/3865679-ram-temple-enforces-religious-restrictions-amid-controversy).
- 2024 — Madras HC asked TN HR&CE to frame an SOP for weddings inside temples, observing that the ceremony can be conducted inside the temple but dining should be elsewhere (TN, cited only as the one judicial statement on temple-wedding SOPs) — [The Hindu via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-hindu-erode-9WW6/20240315/281552295836070).
- 2021 — Odisha Endowments Commission advisory (at Law Dept request) requires all shrines under its jurisdiction to verify age documents of bride and groom before allowing a wedding; Collectors to ensure the same at non-Endowment shrines — [OdishaBytes, 21 Sep 2021](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-temples-barred-from-solemnising-child-marriages/amp) (older than 2024; included because it confirms temple weddings are an established practice in Odisha and that a state-wide document-check rule exists).
- Policy driver: PM Modi's Dec 2023 "Wed in India" appeal at the Uttarakhand Investors' Summit (target of 5,000 destination weddings in the state within a year) and CM Dhami's statement that the state gives "all possible support" to temple weddings — [Swarajya, Apr 2024](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married); [Ghughuti, May 2025](https://ghughuti.org/global-couples-flock-to-triyuginarayan-for-divine-weddings-over-500-tied-the-knot-this-year/).

### Inferences
- A wedding (Hindu or otherwise) at any ASI-protected non-living monument (Konark, Khajuraho, Martand, Hampi-type sites) is effectively impossible for a private family: the only exemption is for "recognized religious functions" at living shrines, and even then the ASI circle must be approached in writing.
- Where ASI protects a *living* temple (e.g., parts of Lingaraj complex, Sun Temple Modhera, some Khajuraho shrines), the question becomes whether a wedding counts as a "recognized religious function"; no source resolves this, so treat it as case-by-case with the ASI circle.
- The Shimla case is the clearest 2026 statement of a common unwritten rule in north-Indian temple halls: non-Hindu ceremonies in trust halls are contentious; the compromise was "social function only, no rites".

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full operative text of Rule 7 (post-2008 amendment) from an official PDF (ASI Chennai PDF returned 503).
- No source found for any ASI "wedding permission" ever granted at a protected monument in 2024–26.

## Key question 1 — Uttarakhand (Triyuginarayan, Omkareshwar Ukhimath, Haridwar, Rishikesh)

### Takeaway
Triyuginarayan (Rudraprayag) is the one north-Indian temple with a formal, state-promoted wedding regime: weddings are solemnised **(a) inside the complex** around the Akhand Dhuni, permission comes from the Shri Badrinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee (BKTC) via an offline application (Kedarnath EO as nodal officer), registration is through the local Tirth Purohit Samiti (Rs 1,100 cited by planners; no official tariff published), and BKTC's do's-and-don'ts ban DJs, alcohol, drones, firecrackers and brass bands with ~50-guest parties. Volume: ~600 weddings in 2024 and ~500 by April 2025. BKTC's Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath (Kedarnath's winter seat) was opened as a second venue in April 2024 via its Usha-Anirudh mandap. Haridwar/Rishikesh weddings happen at ashrams (Parmarth Niketan, Shantikunj), not at Har Ki Pauri, which is Ganga Sabha-controlled with a non-Hindu entry ban.

### Cited Findings
**Triyuginarayan**
- Administering authority: Sri Badarinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee (BKTC); it "has drawn up a list of do's and don'ts for weddings at the two temples, modeled on the rules followed at Shantikunj Haridwar" — [Swarajya, Apr 2024 (updated Dec 2024)](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married). BKTC's own website does not mention Triyuginarayan weddings at all (it only says BKTC manages 45 other temples; office enquiry 0135-2741600) — [badrinath-kedarnath.gov.in](https://badrinath-kedarnath.gov.in/).
- Permission/booking: "Those wishing to organize a marriage at Triyuginarayan must get permission from the Shri Badrinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee"; applications are offline, with the executive officer of Kedarnath temple as nodal officer and the Triyuginarayan temple manager as assistant nodal officer; an online process was planned but not started — [Humari Baat (news)](https://humaribaat.in/news/religion/triyuginarayan-will-now-be-available-as-a-wedding-location/amp) (page now redirects; quoted from search snippet — treat as mid-2024 reporting).
- Registration fee: planners state Rs 1,100 registration with the Tirtha Purohit Samiti, no online registration, Aadhaar copy taken — [Rishikesh Day Tour (planner)](https://rishikeshdaytour.com/blog/triyuginarayan-temple-wedding-cost); [differ.blog (planner)](https://differ.blog/p/triyuginarayan-temple-wedding-registration-online-possible-or-do-co-e4dac7). Another planner estimates Rs 10,000–15,000 for "temple booking/Samiti registration" and says book 30–60 days ahead (3–6 months for peak muhurats), first-come-first-served — [Chardham Tour (planner)](https://www.chardhamtour.in/tourism/wedding-at-triyuginarayan-temple/). **These figures conflict and none is from BKTC.**
- Where the rite happens: saat pheras at the sacred fire altar inside the temple complex, then pag phera around the eternal flame; "Weddings at the temple follow Vedic traditions and require prior registration. Ceremonies must take place in the presence of parents or guardians"; all other functions at nearby hotels/resorts — [Ghughuti, 10 May 2025](https://ghughuti.org/global-couples-flock-to-triyuginarayan-for-divine-weddings-over-500-tied-the-knot-this-year/).
- Volume: ~500 weddings by April 2025 vs 600 in all of 2024; 100+ per month in season — [Ghughuti](https://ghughuti.org/global-couples-flock-to-triyuginarayan-for-divine-weddings-over-500-tied-the-knot-this-year/).
- Season/timings: "wedding window" November–April when the vivah mandap is open for Vedic rites; pre-wedding functions at the party's place of stay — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married). Planners recommend May–June and Sept–Oct and warn against July–Aug monsoon — [Chardham Tour](https://www.chardhamtour.in/tourism/wedding-at-triyuginarayan-temple/). (Conflict: Swarajya's Nov–Apr window vs planners' May–Oct; likely the temple is open year-round but BKTC's formal "window" statement is from the 2024 article.)
- Conditions (BKTC suggested norms as reported): no DJs, no alcohol, no drones, no firecrackers, no brass bands; parties of 30–50, "not more than 50"; local food on leaf plates; traditional instruments and oil lamps — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married). "No DJ music is allowed inside the premises"; photography/drone are planner add-ons — [Chardham Tour](https://www.chardhamtour.in/tourism/wedding-at-triyuginarayan-temple/). Photo/video "requires authorization from the temple committee" — [differ.blog](https://differ.blog/p/everything-you-need-to-know-about-getting-married-at-triyuginarayan-te-bb61c0).
- Legal: the temple-committee certificate "is NOT a legal certificate"; register at Rudraprayag SDM/registrar (fee Rs 100–500, 3 witnesses, affidavits) — [SacredYatra (planner)](https://tours.sacredyatra.com/?p=4950).
- Infrastructure: BKTC renovation in three phases with a 2025 target (DPR, water passage, admin block and a hall at Triyuginarayan); Omkareshwar works Rs 470.39 lakh under a 2023 MoU with a southern media house — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married).
- Package costs (planners, for context only): Rs 51,000–1.2 lakh for a ceremony-only package; Rs 1.05 lakh starting packages; total 2.5–6 lakh with stay; priests Rs 3,000–10,000 — [Rishikesh Day Tour](https://rishikeshdaytour.com/blog/triyuginarayan-temple-wedding-cost); [differ.blog](https://differ.blog/p/triyuginarayan-temple-booking-for-marriage-packages-start-1-05l-b2ab48).

**Omkareshwar Temple, Ukhimath (BKTC)**
- First wedding (Delhi couple Lakhan Chandela and Sangeeta Singh) in April 2024 at the temple's Usha-Anirudh wedding pavilion, promoted by the BKTC chairman as a new venue — [Curly Tales, 19 Apr 2024](https://curlytales.com/omkareshwar-9-other-temples-in-india-that-are-popular-wedding-venues/); ANI report (403 on fetch) — [ANI mirror](https://aniweb.voxturr.us/news/national/general-news/omkareshwar-temple-debuts-as-wedding-venue-after-delhi-couple-tie-knot20240418214022).
- Nov 2025: BKTC chairman Hemant Dwivedi: "We are upgrading facilities here under the state's religious tourism development plan"; local priest says weddings "have grown manifold"; temple courtyards' open pavilions used as mandaps; parties book dharamshalas/guesthouses for low-budget weddings and hire Rishikesh/Dehradun firms for larger ones — [ETV Bharat, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/offbeat/uttarakhand-ukhimath-emerges-as-a-divine-wedding-destination-after-triyuginarayan-enn25111101463).

**Haridwar / Rishikesh**
- Har Ki Pauri is managed by the Ganga Sabha (1916 bylaws); Jan 2026 signs declare the area "non-Hindu prohibited"; no source describes any permit route for weddings on the ghat — [IANS, 16 Jan 2026](https://ianslive.in/in-haridwar-posters-barring-non-hindus-from-entry-come-up-at-har-ki-pauri--20260116194204); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/culture/non-hindu-prohibited-area-signs-installed-at-har-ki-pauri-in-haridwar).
- Parmarth Niketan (Rishikesh): "Individuals and families also regularly come to Parmarth to perform sacred rites ceremonies (sanskaras)" including "sacred weddings" — [sadhviji.org](https://www.sadhviji.org/seva/parmarth). No published fee/booking page; a travel blog describes a wedding held at Parmarth's own Ganga ghat — [Tripoto](https://www.tripoto.com/uttarakhand/trips/haridwar-spiritual-vibes-and-a-wedding-5a2ba06c96cc4).
- Shantikunj (Gayatri Pariwar, Haridwar): runs free mass weddings for poor families (37 marriages on one Dussehra) and is the model for BKTC's rulebook — [Tribune archive](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/community/gayatri-shantikunj-marries-off-37-poor-149344); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married).

### Inferences
- Triyuginarayan is category (a) with a two-layer process: BKTC permission (free as far as reported) + Tirth Purohit Samiti registration/dakshina (Rs 1,100 widely quoted). The "Rs 10–15k" figure on planner sites almost certainly bundles planner margins.
- Omkareshwar Ukhimath is category (a)/(b) under the same BKTC regime; expect the same do's-and-don'ts.
- Haridwar/Rishikesh are category (c): ashram weddings (Parmarth, Shantikunj) rather than temple weddings; Har Ki Pauri should be listed as NO.

### Gaps
- No official BKTC tariff, form, or rulebook text is online; BKTC's own site is silent. Numbers of weddings at Ukhimath are not published.
- Parmarth Niketan's wedding donation/procedure is not published anywhere I could find.

## Key question 2 — Maharashtra and Goa

### Takeaway
Goa is the clearest "yes": GSB Saunsthans run marriage halls on-campus with published tariffs (Shantadurga Kavale: ~Rs 5,700/day, advance, non-refundable; Mahajan/Kulavi-only room booking) and the Fatorpa Shantadurga and Mangeshi halls are listed on wedding portals (500 and ~120–150 pax). In Maharashtra, the big pilgrimage trusts (Siddhivinayak, Shirdi, Trimbakeshwar, Pandharpur, Kolhapur, Tuljapur, Jejuri, Shani Shingnapur) publish no wedding service; ISKCON Juhu is the only Mumbai temple with a documented on-campus wedding/banquet practice. Nothing confirms Siddhivinayak mass weddings.

### Cited Findings

- Shri Shantadurga Fatarpekarin Temple, Fatorpa (Cuncolim): "Shree Shantadurga Faterpekarin Sabhagrah", one hall, up to 500 guests, 100-car parking, 2 changing rooms; alcohol not allowed; veg and non-veg allowed, outside caterers and decorators allowed; DJ policy to be discussed; no overnight events; 30% deposit — [The Wedding Company listing](https://www.theweddingcompany.com/wedding-venues/cuncolim/shri-shantadurga-fatarpekarin-temple-fatorpa) (portal listing, not temple site).
