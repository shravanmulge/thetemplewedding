# Temples in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana that host weddings (2024–2026)

Researcher note on sourcing: official pages from TTD (tirumala.org, news.tirumala.org) and mainstream newspapers (The Hans India, Deccan Chronicle, The Federal, The Hindu via inkl) were reachable. The AP Endowments (aptemples.ap.gov.in) and Telangana Endowments temple sites did not surface any kalyana-mandapam tariff or booking page in search. A very large share of the "temple wedding" information online for AP/Telangana comes from three third-party travel/booking blogs (gotirupati.com, prayanamam.com, gokshetra.com), which are inconsistent with each other and often undated or from 2019–2023; several of their pages also returned HTTP 403 when fetched. Figures from those blogs are marked "(third-party, unverified)" below and should be treated as indicative only. The same phone number (85000 87012) appears on blog pages for Dwaraka Tirumala, Srisailam, Bhadrachalam and Yadadri, which suggests it is the blog's own helpline rather than a temple number.

A legend used throughout: (a) = wedding inside the temple complex itself; (b) = temple-run / devasthanam-run kalyana mandapam adjacent to or in the town; (c) = only private halls nearby.

## Key Question 1: TTD – kalyana mandapams, rents/deposits, booking process, and whether weddings are allowed on Tirumala hill

### Takeaway
TTD runs a network of roughly 250–280 kalyana mandapams (about 180–210 in AP, 65–74 in Telangana, plus a handful in other states), bookable online through its seva portal, with an official tariff that is tiered by town size; on Tirumala hill itself private weddings are NOT held inside or in front of the Srivari temple, but TTD does solemnise large numbers of free, parent-attended, first-time Hindu weddings at its Kalyana Vedika on Papavinasanam Road (26,777 by end-2025), and paid weddings are also performed through the Tirumala Purohit Sangham / TTD mandapams on the hill.

**Weddings on Tirumala hill**
- Free weddings: TTD's Kalyana Vedika on Papavinasanam Road, Tirumala, opened 25 Apr 2016; 26,777 marriages solemnised through 31 Dec 2025 (2,731 in 2016–17; 4,705 in 2017–18; 5,047 in 2018–19; 91 in 2020–21). TTD provides free priest, mangala vadyam, turmeric/kumkum/sacred threads; couples bring everything else. Eligibility: Hindus only; bride 18+, groom 21+; first marriages only — "love marriages and second marriages are not conducted"; parents of both must attend or submit documents. Booking online via the TTD website (online slot booking began 9 May 2016) with Aadhaar and age proof; acknowledgement slip; obtain an "unmarried" certificate from MRO/tahsildar; report 6 hours before the muhurtam for document verification; Rs 50/night accommodation subject to availability; a Hindu Marriage Sub-Registrar office is inside the Vedika (10:30–17:00). After the wedding, six persons get Rs 300 special-entry darshan free and free laddus. — [The Hans India, 21 Jan 2026](https://assets.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/tirumala-turns-preferred-wedding-venue-for-new-couples-1040966)
- Same facility: 26,214 weddings to 1 May 2025; contact 0877-2263433; booking via ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in. — [The Hans India, 5 May 2025](https://thehansindia.com/news/cities/tirupathi/ttd-kalyana-vedika-a-boon-for-parents-968309); [Deccan Chronicle, 4 May 2025](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/newlyweds-applaud-tirumala-kalyana-vedika-facilities-ttd-1876858)

### Inferences
- TTD mandapams fall under category (b): they are town halls run by TTD (often far from Tirupati), not weddings inside TTD temples. Weddings on Tirumala hill are category (b) too — at the Kalyana Vedika (free) or Purohit Sangham/TTD mandapams — never inside the Srivari temple (category (a) is not available at Tirumala).
- The tirumala.org tariff (Rs 500–5,000) is clearly stale given the Rs 9,440 → Rs 50,000 Visakhapatnam hike and the Rs 1.1–1.4 lakh figures quoted by Tirupati guides; actual rents must be read off the booking portal for each hall.
- TTD's 2025 "underutilisation" finding implies that outside Tirupati and the big cities many TTD mandapams are available at short notice.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) consolidated TTD rent/deposit schedule could be retrieved; the portal requires login and no board resolution with a rent table surfaced. Deposit amounts were not found in any source.
- Capacity figures for individual TTD halls are available only from third-party blogs.
- Could not retrieve the Purohit Sangham's current fee schedule for paid weddings on Tirumala, nor an explicit official TTD rule text stating that private weddings inside the temple are prohibited (the prohibition appears only in third-party guides and by implication from the Kalyana Vedika/Purohit Sangham arrangement).
- The Feb 2026 TTD notice listing "Marriage – Free of cost" among Priests' Association services was mentioned in a search summary but no URL was returned.

## Key Question 2: Other Andhra Pradesh temples (AP Endowments or trust-run) where weddings are held

### Takeaway
Several major AP Endowments temples host devotee weddings in devasthanam-run mandapams or pillared halls next to the temple (Annavaram, Dwaraka Tirumala, Srisailam, Simhachalam, Ahobilam, Mahanandi, Penchalakona), typically restricted to Hindu arranged marriages with bride 18+/groom 21+ and with modest fees; however, almost all tariff and booking detail available online is from third-party blogs, and no AP Endowments statewide mandapam booking portal or tariff was found.

### Cited Findings

**AP Endowments Department portal**
- Srisailam temple takes seva bookings only through the Endowments portal aptemples.ap.gov.in or the devasthanam's site; the Mangalagiri Panakala Swamy kalyanam seva is also booked via the AP Temples portal. No search result showed a kalyana-mandapam booking function or tariff on aptemples.ap.gov.in. — [Deccan Chronicle: Srisailam online seva](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/srisailam-temple-introduces-online-seva-booking-1924569); [GoKshetra: Panakala Swamy](https://gokshetra.com/panakala-swamy-kalyanam-booking/)
- AP Endowments officials opened a kalyana mandapam at the Atmakur (Nellore) temple to facilitate a monthly Kalyanotsavam (undated). — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/kalyana-mandapam-to-facilitate-monthly-kalyanotsavam-1979630)

- Vontimitta: only the annual TTD-run Sri Rama Kalyanam (state festival) and Srinivasa Kalyanam are reported; no devotee-wedding information. — [HinduPad](https://hindupad.com/srinivasa-kalyanam-in-ttd-temples/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/content/465646/ap-celebrate-ramanavami-vontimitta-not.html)
- Samarlakota: TTD lists a kalyana mandapam there. — [TTD directory](https://www.tirumala.org/ttdklmps.aspx)
- Mantralayam (Sri Raghavendra Swamy Mutt, Kurnool district, private mutt) is widely marketed as a wedding venue by the same blogs, with hall rates quoted at ~Rs 10,000–35,400 plus food. (third-party, unverified) — [gotirupati](https://gotirupati.com/?p=54212)

### Inferences
- Across AP Endowments temples the de-facto standard conditions are: Hindu marriages only, arranged (parental consent), bride 18+ / groom 21+, Aadhaar of couple and parents, families arrange purohit/food/décor themselves, a devasthanam receipt serves as evidence for civil registration (Simhachalam is unusual in issuing a certificate for Rs 250).
- Dwaraka Tirumala and Lower Ahobilam are the clearest examples of category (a) weddings held physically within the temple precincts (pillared portions / "inside the temple during opening hours"); Annavaram, Srisailam, Simhachalam, Mahanandi, Penchalakona are category (b).
- Kanipakam appears to be category (c) in practice (weddings in private halls nearby), despite being a wedding-popular temple.

### Gaps
- No official AP Endowments or individual devasthanam web page with a kalyana mandapam tariff, capacity, timings or online booking was retrievable for any of these temples; all numbers are third-party and mostly 2019–2023.
- No information at all was found for Draksharamam, Ryali, Antarvedi, Puttaparthi, Tiruchanur, Appalayagunta or Tirupati Govindaraja Swamy regarding weddings.
- Dress-code, photography, sound and décor rules were not published by any AP devasthanam source found; the only sound rule found is TTD's DJ ban (third-party).

## Key Question 3: Telangana temples where weddings are held, and Telangana Endowments booking/tariffs

### Takeaway
Telangana Endowments temples at Yadadri and Bhadrachalam (and devasthanam satrams at Vemulawada, Komuravelli etc.) host devotee weddings in halls at the foot of the hill or in the town rather than in the sanctum complex, booked offline at the temple office; the Telangana Endowments department offers online arjitha-seva booking at 38 temples but no online kalyana-mandapam booking or published tariff was found, and no reliable evidence was found that Chilkur Balaji, Birla Mandir or ISKCON Hyderabad rent wedding halls, while ASI-protected Ramappa and Thousand Pillar temples are governed by the monument rules that bar functions without central permission.

### Inferences
- In Telangana, devotee weddings at the big Endowments temples are offline-booked, hall-based (category (b)) and moderately priced (five figures in rupees), with the same Hindu/arranged/18-21 conditions as AP; the hilltop Yadadri temple itself does not host weddings.
- Families wanting an online-bookable temple-affiliated hall in Hyderabad or district towns in Telangana are more likely to find one through TTD's 65–74 Telangana mandapams than through the Endowments department.
- Weddings at ASI-protected Kakatiya temples should be assumed not permitted absent written central permission.

### Gaps
- No official Telangana Endowments tariff, booking page or rule text for any kalyana mandapam was found; Yadadri and Bhadrachalam fees are from blogs with conflicting figures.
- Policies of Chilkur Balaji, Birla Mandir and ISKCON Hyderabad on devotee weddings could not be verified from any primary or news source.
- Capacity, timings, dress code, photography and sound rules for Telangana temple halls are unpublished in sources found.
