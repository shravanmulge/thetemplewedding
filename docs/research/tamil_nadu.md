# Tamil Nadu and Puducherry temples that host weddings (2024–2026)

**Source-quality note for the writer.** The official Tamil Nadu HR&CE sites (hrce.tn.gov.in, tnhrce.gov.in and the per-temple subdomains such as maduraimeenakshi.hrce.tn.gov.in, tiruchendurmurugan.hrce.tn.gov.in, vadapalanimurugan.hrce.tn.gov.in, thiruparankundrammurugan.hrce.tn.gov.in) could not be reached from this research environment (connection reset / HTTP 503 on every attempt), so no fee or rule below is confirmed against an official HR&CE page. The best-quality sources found are (a) news reports (The Hindu via inkl, DT Next, Deccan Chronicle, Mylapore Times, The South First, Organiser) and (b) the Madras High Court's 2025 marriage-hall judgment. Fee figures come almost entirely from three Indian pilgrimage blogs (gotirupati.com, prayanamam.com, gokshetra.com), which are secondary, partly undated, internally inconsistent and sometimes contradict each other; every figure from them is flagged "(blog, unverified)". Families should treat them as order-of-magnitude leads only and confirm with the temple office.

## Key question 1: Does TN HR&CE publish a list of temple kalyana mandapams with tariffs and online booking? What is the process, typical rent, and documents?

### Takeaway
No central HR&CE list of temple marriage halls with tariffs, and no central online marriage-hall booking module, was found; all sources describe booking in person at the individual temple office (a few blogs vaguely say "Tamil Nadu residents can register online" without naming a portal). Documents are fairly standardised across HR&CE temples (Aadhaar of couple and both sets of parents, age proof/TC, community certificate, an "unmarried certificate" from TN e-Sevai replacing the old VAO "first-marriage certificate", plus police NOC and notarised parental consent for inter-caste weddings), and the temple issues a receipt/certificate that is used for later civil registration.

### Cited Findings
- A Palani kalyana-mandapam guide states that "charges for the hall and the ritual are not published on any official central portal and vary by temple, hall size, day and the scale of the function", and that for HR&CE temples tnhrce.gov.in is only a reference for which temples the department administers — [Prayanamam – Palani Kalyana Mandapam](https://prayanamam.com/palani-kalyana-mandapam-booking-process-marriage-hall-cost) (blog; published Feb 2023, updated Dec 2025)
- The same guide says "most of these temples handle such bookings through their own marriage office rather than a central online system" — [Prayanamam – Palani](https://prayanamam.com/palani-kalyana-mandapam-booking-process-marriage-hall-cost)
- The HR&CE online portal is described (for Tiruvannamalai, Tiruchendur, Palani) as covering darshan, abhishekam and seva tickets; no source found says marriage halls can be booked on it — [Tirthayatra – online ticket booking for Tiruvannamalai, Tiruchendur, Palani](https://www.tirthayatra.org/?p=38260); [Tiruvannamalai Devasthanam online booking page](https://tiruvannamalaidevasthanam.org.in/tiruvannamalai-temple-online-booking/)
- A Go Kshetra guide for Tiruvannamalai states there is "no verified private-wedding booking page or online booking" and advises starting with the HR&CE temple contact page and avoiding third-party planners; it also says do not treat old blog prices as confirmed — [Go Kshetra – Tiruvannamalai marriage rules](https://gokshetra.com/tiruvannamalai-temple-marriage-procedure-rules-registration/) (dated Aug 2023)
- Two blog pages for HR&CE temples (Devanathaswamy, Thiruvanthipuram; and "Arupadai Veedu Murugan" temple) state that "online marriage registration is only available for Tamil Nadu residents" but do not name any portal; the only online element actually described is applying for the Unmarried Certificate on TN e-Sevai — [GoTirupati – Devanathaswamy temple marriage registration](https://gotirupati.com/?p=63678); [GoTirupati – Arupadai Veedu Murugan temple marriage hall](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64007) (blogs, undated)
- **Documents (HR&CE policy, as reported by The Hindu via inkl):** HR&CE "has done away with the need to submit first-wedding certificate from Revenue department for marriages conducted in temples"; Minister P.K. Sekarbabu said couples must now submit the 'unmarried' status certificate in its place, obtainable at e-Seva centres; he said "only couples who were Hindus and Indian nationals were allowed to marry in temples" and that they must submit address proof, ID proof and Aadhaar — [The Hindu via inkl – HR&CE makes changes in certificates to be submitted for weddings in temples](https://www.inkl.com/news/hr-ce-makes-changes-in-certificates-to-be-submitted-for-weddings-in-temples) (article date not visible in search results; page returned 403 on fetch)
- A revenue-department directive reportedly told VAOs to stop issuing the old "first marriage certificate" (never an official revenue document) and advised HR&CE temples not to insist on it; the unmarried certificate is issued by tahsildars with digital signature — [search-result snippet attributed to a secondary page](https://candidharmonyaerofly.pages.dev/584105.html) (low-quality page; corroborated in substance by the inkl/Hindu report)
- The Unmarried Certificate is service code REV-120 on the Tamil Nadu e-Sevai portal — [TN e-Sevai](https://tnesevai.tn.gov.in/Default.aspx); [OBC Rights guide to Unmarried Certificate in TN](https://obcrights.org/blog/certificates/revenue-certificates/unmarried-certificate/ways-to-apply-for-an-unmarried-certificate-in-tamil-nadu/)
- Blog pages report that the unmarried certificate takes about 10–15 days to issue — [GoTirupati – Devanathaswamy](https://gotirupati.com/?p=63678)
- **Standard document set reported across HR&CE temples (blogs):** Aadhaar photocopies of bride, groom and both sets of parents; 10th mark sheet or Transfer Certificate; community (caste) certificate; unmarried certificate from TN e-Sevai or an application signed/sealed by the Tahsildar/MRO; for inter-caste weddings a police NOC and a notarised document recording both sets of parents' consent; wedding invitation card and photos for the post-wedding receipt — [Prayanamam – Tiruchendur mandapam booking](https://prayanamam.com/?p=2425) (Mar 2023); [GoTirupati – Madurai Meenakshi marriage](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64010) (Mar 2023); [GoTirupati – Arupadai Veedu](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64007)
- An older Hindu-Blog post says the basic rules are "displayed on notice boards at important temples in Tamil Nadu": birth certificates, address proof, age proof, a Tahsildar-attested first-marriage document (now superseded, see above) and parental consent letters — [Hindu Blog – Documents and rules for temple wedding in TN](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2014/05/documents-and-rules-and-regulations-for.html) (2014; outdated on the first-marriage certificate point)
- **Temple-run mandapam rents found (all blog, unverified):** Tiruchendur Rs 2,500–4,500 per hall-day; Tiruttani "Rs 5,000 onwards" / Rs 8,000–20,000; Srirangam Rs 5,000–25,000; Palani Rs 5,000–45,000 (another blog: Rs 15,000–40,000 for "temple halls"); Mylapore Kapaleeswarar's Greenways Road hall Rs 7.5 lakh + GST (= Rs 8.85 lakh) per 24 h in 2019 (news) — see per-temple sections below for URLs
- **Marriage "permission/enrolment" fee for a wedding inside the temple (blog, unverified):** Vadapalani Rs 2,000; Thiruporur Rs 2,000 + Rs 100 form; Kundrathur about Rs 1,000; Marudamalai (Coimbatore) Rs 250; Madurai Meenakshi Rs 20,000 incl. purohit and nadaswaram (one blog) vs Rs 1,000–2,500 booking fee + Rs 2,000–15,000 hall (another blog) — see per-temple sections
- **New HR&CE marriage-hall programme struck down:** HR&CE Minister P.K. Sekarbabu's budget announcement of marriage halls in 27 temples at about Rs 80 crore from surplus temple funds was quashed by the Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court (Justices S.M. Subramaniam and G. Arul Murugan), order dated 19 August 2025 (reported 26 Aug), holding that building halls for rent is not a "religious purpose" under the HR&CE Act 1959 and that temple funds belong to the deity — [The South First – Government cannot use temple funds to build marriage halls](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/government-cannot-use-temple-funds-to-build-marriage-halls-madras-hc/); [Verdictum – Rama Ravikumar v State of TN](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/madras-high-court/madurai-rama-ravikumar-v-the-state-of-tamil-nadu-govt-orders-marriage-halls-construction-from-temple-funds-1589728); [Organiser – 29 Aug 2025](https://organiser.org/2025/08/29/313067/bharat/tamil-nadu-madras-hc-slams-dmk-govt-says-temple-funds-not-public-money-stalls-plan-to-divert-them-for-commercial-use)
- The Supreme Court on 16 Sept 2025 declined to stay the High Court order, remarking that devotees don't offer money to temples for setting up marriage halls — [The Week (PTI) – 16 Sep 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/09/16/lgd14-sc-temples-tamil-nadu.html); [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/national/devotees-dont-offer-money-to-temples-for-setting-up-marriage-halls-sc-846765)
- On the HR&CE Commissioner's letter of 15 June 2026, the TN government dropped the marriage-hall and commercial-complex projects for 22 listed temples from its Assembly announcements; work had not begun — [The South First – 20 Jun 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/tamil-nadu-government-drops-temple-marriage-hall-and-commercial-complex-projects-from-assembly-announcements-following-hc-order/)
- The Madras High Court has separately directed HR&CE to fix/revise rents on temple properties every three years — [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/rent-temple-properties-madras-high-court)

### Inferences
- Because HR&CE's central e-services cover darshan/seva tickets and not halls, families should expect to book weddings in person at each temple's office; "online booking" headings on blogs are mostly SEO filler (several pages have the heading but describe only in-person steps).
- The 2025 HC ruling freezes expansion of temple-owned commercial kalyana mandapams, so the stock of temple-run halls (Tiruchendur, Palani, Tiruttani, Srirangam, Mylapore etc.) is unlikely to grow in 2025–2026; existing halls continue to operate.
- The document regime is effectively uniform across HR&CE temples because it flows from the Commissioner's circulars (e.g., the switch from "first marriage certificate" to e-Sevai unmarried certificate), so a family can prepare one standard dossier.

### Gaps
- Could not verify any fee, hall name or rule on hrce.tn.gov.in or the per-temple HR&CE subdomains (unreachable from this environment). The report-writer should flag all tariffs as unverified blog figures.
- Date of the HR&CE "unmarried certificate" policy change was not visible (The Hindu article accessible only via inkl, which returned 403 on fetch; likely 2023 based on surrounding sources but not confirmed).
- No source found that enumerates which HR&CE temples own kalyana mandapams, or any published HR&CE tariff schedule.

## Key question 2: Meenakshi Amman Temple, Madurai — are weddings performed inside? Where? Booking and fees?

### Takeaway
Sources agree weddings are conducted on the temple premises (in a "sannathi"/mandapam area, not before the main Meenakshi deity), booked in person at the temple office, with a cap of about 2–3 weddings a day, after 6 am, 1–2 hours each, Hindus only, arranged marriages with parental consent; but the fee figures conflict badly (Rs 20,000 all-in vs Rs 10,000–15,000 "simple wedding" built from Rs 1,000–2,500 booking + hall + priest) and the named mandapams (Meenakshi Nayakkar, Thirukalyana, Vasantha) appear only in one 2026 blog with no official attribution.

### Cited Findings
- Marriages "are not held before the main deity Meenakshi Amman"; they take place "inside Sannathi"; 2–3 marriages per day are allowed; marriages allowed only after 6:00 am; each lasts max 1–2 hours — [GoTirupati – Madurai Meenakshi Temple Marriage Procedure](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64010) (blog, published 8 Mar 2023)
- Fee: Rs 20,000 covering marriage fee, purohit charges and nadaswaram; pooja articles, decoration and other services extra; application form collected from the temple office by the bride's/groom's family 2–3 weeks in advance; no online booking described — [GoTirupati – Madurai Meenakshi](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64010) (blog, unverified)
- Eligibility per the same page: bride ≥18, groom ≥21, both Hindu, "only arranged marriages", no inter-religious marriages, no love marriages without parental acceptance; inter-caste arranged marriages need police NOC and notarised parental-consent document in addition to Aadhaar (couple + both sets of parents), unmarried certificate or Tahsildar-sealed application, community certificate and TC — [GoTirupati – Madurai Meenakshi](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64010)
- The same page contradictorily says "marriage halls are located outside the temple and are 2 to 3 km from the main temple" — [GoTirupati – Madurai Meenakshi](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64010)
- A 2026 Go Kshetra guide names four venues: "Meenakshi Nayakkar Mandapam" (historic, moderate capacity), "Thirukalyana Mandapam" (designed for weddings), "Vasantha Mandapam" (larger) and a "General Mandapam" (lowest cost); gives approximate 2026 costs: non-refundable booking fee Rs 1,000–2,500; basic hall (2–3 hrs) Rs 2,000–5,000; premium hall Rs 5,000–15,000; priest Rs 2,000–5,000; refundable security deposit Rs 2,000–5,000; total Rs 10,000–50,000+, simple wedding ~Rs 10,000–15,000 — [Go Kshetra – Meenakshi Amman Temple Marriage Guide](https://gokshetra.com/meenakshi-amman-temple-marriage-guide/) (blog, dated 4 Apr 2026; attributes rules to "temple administration" but cites no official document; **no numeric capacities given**)
- Same guide: booking in person at the temple administration office inside the complex; book 2–3 months ahead (4–6 in peak season); slots morning 5–10 am (most popular), 10 am–12 pm, evening 4–7 pm; ceremony ~2–3 hours; dress code groom veshti/angavastram, bride silk saree, guests no shorts/sleeveless, footwear removed; photography needs prior permission and a separate fee, photographers need ID and permission letter, no drones/tripods/elaborate lighting, no photos in the inner sanctum; concessions for poorer families case-by-case; temple trust sometimes holds mass weddings — [Go Kshetra – Meenakshi guide 2026](https://gokshetra.com/meenakshi-amman-temple-marriage-guide/)
- An earlier Go Kshetra page (Sept 2023) says couples can marry "within its sacred premises" but also that "there are no marriage halls within the temple premises", lists no fees, and says no information is available on online marriage booking — [Go Kshetra – Madurai Meenakshi marriage procedure rules](https://gokshetra.com/madurai-meenakshi-amman-temple-marriage-procedure-rules) (blog)
- Taj Gateway Hotels (Madurai) runs a marketing blog on the significance of a temple wedding at Meenakshi temple (page returned 403; content not verified) — [Gateway Hotels blog](https://www.gateway-hotels.com/en-in/blog/wedding-and-events/why-a-temple-wedding-at-meenakshi-temple-madurai-is-significant)
- The annual celestial wedding (Meenakshi Thirukalyanam, Chithirai festival) is a public festival with no booking system for participation — [Go Kshetra – celestial wedding](https://gokshetra.com/madurai-meenakshi-celestial-wedding-online-booking-story)

### Inferences
- The consistent elements (in-person booking at temple office, Hindus only, arranged marriage, 2–3 per day, morning slots, short ceremony, documents) are likely accurate HR&CE practice because they match what the same blogs and news report for other HR&CE temples.
- The two fee pictures are incompatible; the Rs 20,000 "package" (2023) may include purohit/nadaswaram, whereas the 2026 itemised ranges may describe cheaper bare-bones options. Neither is official.
- The mandapam names in the 2026 guide are unverified; "Meenakshi Nayakkar Mandapam" is a historic pillared hall at the east entrance and may not be bookable. Treat as leads.

### Gaps
- No official Meenakshi temple/HR&CE statement on which mandapam hosts private weddings, the daily quota, or fees (maduraimeenakshi.hrce.tn.gov.in unreachable).
- No capacity figures; no source on separate temple-run kalyana mandapams in Madurai city (the GoTirupati "2–3 km away" halls are not identified or confirmed as temple-owned).

## Key question 3: Other major Tamil Nadu temples (south and central TN)

### Takeaway
Weddings inside the temple complex plus temple-run halls are documented (by news and blogs) at Tiruchendur, Thiruparankundram, Thiruporur, Tiruttani, Srirangam, Swamimalai and Thirukadaiyur; Palani halls exist but are poorly documented; Tiruvannamalai and Srivilliputhur are reported as not offering private weddings; Chidambaram (Dikshitar-run, not HR&CE) holds weddings only in the Pandiyanayakar Sannathi; Thanjavur Brihadeeswarar/Gangaikonda Cholapuram/Darasuram/Mahabalipuram produced no evidence of weddings being hosted.

- Capacity is managed by advance "booking/registration" (Thiruporur: 72 of 102 booked); unregistered couples are still accommodated informally at the sanctum on peak days, implying the "quota" is not strictly enforced at every temple.

## Key question 6: Rules on non-Hindus, inter-caste, love marriages, registration under the Hindu Marriage Act, and "temple wedding certificates"

### Takeaway
HR&CE policy (as stated by the Minister and enforced by temple EOs) is that only Hindu Indian nationals may marry in HR&CE temples; inter-caste Hindu arranged marriages are allowed with extra documents (police NOC, notarised parental consent) at most temples, though a few (e.g., Devanathaswamy) reportedly refuse them; "love marriages" without parental acceptance are widely refused; the temple's receipt/certificate is not a legal marriage certificate and the couple must register separately (Hindu Marriage Act 1955 or TN Registration of Marriages Act 2009) at the Sub-Registrar.

### Cited Findings
- Minister P.K. Sekarbabu: "only couples who were Hindus and Indian nationals were allowed to marry in temples"; address proof, ID proof, Aadhaar and the unmarried certificate (replacing the first-wedding certificate) required; verification continues — [The Hindu via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/hr-ce-makes-changes-in-certificates-to-be-submitted-for-weddings-in-temples)
- HR&CE temple EO circular (Thiruvetteeswarar, Chennai, May 2023): "Only Hindus will be allowed to marry in this temple"; wedding of a Christian couple cancelled as "in violation of HR&CE rules" (rule not specified) — [Organiser, 10 May 2023](https://organiser.org/2023/05/10/173380/bharat/tamil-nadu-following-protests-wedding-of-christian-couple-scheduled-to-take-place-at-temple-gets-cancelled/)
- Inter-caste arranged marriage requirements reported at Madurai Meenakshi, Tiruchendur and "Arupadai Veedu" temple: police NOC for bride and groom plus notarised document of both sets of parents' acceptance; parents must attend the booking; a VRO certificate of parental acceptance at Tiruchendur — [GoTirupati – Madurai Meenakshi](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64010); [GoTirupati – Tiruchendur](https://gotirupati.com/?p=54480); [GoTirupati – Arupadai Veedu](https://gotirupati.com/?p=64007) (blogs)
- Devanathaswamy (Thiruvanthipuram) reportedly allows only same-community marriages; inter-caste and inter-religious not allowed — [GoTirupati – Devanathaswamy](https://gotirupati.com/?p=63678) (blog; conflicts with the inter-caste-allowed practice elsewhere)
- Minimum ages 18 (bride) / 21 (groom) cited at every temple, matching the Hindu Marriage Act — e.g. [Prayanamam – Vadapalani](https://prayanamam.com/?p=5574); [Go Kshetra – Meenakshi 2026](https://gokshetra.com/meenakshi-amman-temple-marriage-guide/)
- The temple issues a receipt/certificate after the wedding; "this certificate is not the same as legal registration under the Hindu Marriage Act" and the couple must register separately — [Prayanamam – Thiruverkadu](https://prayanamam.com/?p=6852); [Hindu Blog 2014](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2014/05/documents-and-rules-and-regulations-for.html); [GoTirupati – Srirangam](https://gotirupati.com/?p=48100) ("marriage registration is not available at the temple")
- Registration route: Hindu Marriage Act 1955 (both parties Hindu/Buddhist/Jain/Sikh) through the TN Registration Department (tnreginet); interfaith marriages go under the Special Marriage Act; TN Registration of Marriages Act 2009 also used — [RegisterKaro – Marriage certificate Tamil Nadu](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/marriage-certificate-tamil-nadu-a-guide); [India.gov.in – search registered marriages under HMA and apply for certificate (TN)](https://www.india.gov.in/category/governance-planning/subcategory/governance/details/search-to-find-marriage-registration-data-and-apply-online-for-marriage-certificate)
- Under the TN 2009 Rules the memorandum (Form I) must carry signatures of the parties, the priest and two witnesses, with identity, age and place-of-marriage evidence; 90 days to file, a further 60 days on payment of extra fee (per blog summary of the Rules) — [Go Kshetra – Tiruvannamalai](https://gokshetra.com/tiruvannamalai-temple-marriage-procedure-rules-registration/)
- Documents that temples/hall managers hand over for registration: receipt, wedding invitation (original + copies), wedding photos, Aadhaar of couple and parents, 10th mark list, two passport photos — [GoTirupati – Tiruchendur](https://gotirupati.com/?p=54480)
- Child-marriage enforcement: Dikshitars at Chidambaram arrested over child-marriage complaints — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/police-arrest-chidambaram-nataraja-temple-dikshitars-over-complaints-of-child-marriage-several-such-incidents-suspected/)
- District Collectors sometimes preside over HR&CE-organised (mass) marriages, e.g. Pudukkottai 11 Sep 2023 — [Pudukkottai district press release](https://pudukkottai.nic.in/pr-679-11092023/)

### Inferences
- "Arranged marriage with parental presence" is a de facto HR&CE requirement rather than a statute; it is enforced through the document list (parents' Aadhaar, parental-consent affidavits, parents signing the temple register).
- Because the temple receipt is only evidence of solemnisation, a couple wanting a legally recognised marriage must budget time for a separate Sub-Registrar filing within 90 days.

### Gaps
- No HR&CE circular text on non-Hindus or inter-caste weddings was retrieved; the policy is known only through the Minister's quoted remarks and one EO circular.
- The exact date of the "unmarried certificate" circular and whether the first-marriage certificate is still demanded by any individual temple in 2025–2026 could not be confirmed.
