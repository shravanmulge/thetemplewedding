# Temple trusts, not monuments, host weddings reliably

A planner can reliably deliver a traditional Hindu wedding at one specific kind of Indian temple: a living shrine run by a trust, devaswom or state endowment office that operates its own booking counter and its own mandapam or hall. That set is dominated by the south and by a few state-backed northern venues: Guruvayur solemnised a record **416 weddings in a single morning on 23 August 2026** ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/08/23/416-weddings-in-seven-hours-guruvayur-temple-sets-new-record.html)); TTD's free Kalyana Vedika on Tirumala has married **26,777 couples since 2016** and TTD runs roughly **250 bookable kalyana mandapams** across AP, Telangana and beyond ([The Hans India](https://assets.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/tirumala-turns-preferred-wedding-venue-for-new-couples-1040966); [TTD](https://news.tirumala.org/online-booking-of-ttd-kalyana-mandapams-facilitataed/)); Tamil Nadu's HR&CE Murugan temples routinely handle **100+ weddings on a single muhurtham day** ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/crowd-surge-at-tiruporur-temple-with-over-100-weddings)); and Uttarakhand's Triyuginarayan, under the Badrinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee, hosted **about 600 weddings in 2024** ([Ghughuti](https://ghughuti.org/global-couples-flock-to-triyuginarayan-for-divine-weddings-over-500-tied-the-knot-this-year/)). The opposite pole is equally clear: Archaeological Survey of India guidelines exclude private applicants and "religious activities" from events at centrally protected monuments, so no planner should list Konark, Belur-Halebidu, Ramappa or the Hampi bazaar mantapas, and even the living Virupaksha temple inside Hampi now permits only feast-free, camera-free rituals ([ThePrint/PTI](https://theprint.in/india/guidelines-in-place-relating-to-use-of-asi-sites-for-holding-cultural-events-govt/1476626); [Hospet Online](https://hospet.online/?p=18065)). Most of India's most famous shrines (Kashi Vishwanath, Ayodhya, Puri, Banke Bihari, Mahakaleshwar, Siddhivinayak, Shirdi, Vaishno Devi) do not host devotee weddings at all and belong on a "darshan only" list. Across every venue that does, the conditions converge: Hindus only, bride 18 and groom 21, parents present or consenting, first marriages, dhoti and saree, restricted photography, no alcohol, no DJ, and a temple receipt that is evidence of solemnisation but never a legal marriage certificate, so civil registration must be built into every package. A crucial caveat for a website: official sources confirm the booking channel and rules at only a handful of venues (TTD, Guruvayur's reported terms, ISKCON Bengaluru, Thiruvambady, Shantadurga Kavale, Somnath, Delhi's Jagannath Mandir); almost every rupee figure for Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, AP and Telangana temples comes from three unaffiliated pilgrimage blogs (gotirupati.com, prayanamam.com, gokshetra.com) that contradict one another, and must be presented as indicative until confirmed by phone with the temple office.

## ASI rules make protected monuments a hard no, and dual-control temples a soft no

The legal boundary that matters most for a planner is the one drawn by the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act 1958 and its 1959 Rules. Rule 7 bars "meetings, receptions, parties, entertainment or conferences" at a protected monument without written Central Government permission, and the ASI's standing guidelines, as stated by the Culture Minister in a March 2023 Lok Sabha reply, go further: the ASI "generally does not permit monuments, or land attached to them, to host functions or events"; only monuments specifically identified for cultural events qualify; events must be "of a high standard, preferably classical"; **"commercial or religious activities" are prohibited**; and **only government departments and public bodies may apply, with private individuals and commercial organisations excluded**. Functions must end by 10 pm with no loudspeakers after, temporary structures must be removed the same day, and a refundable deposit of Rs 50,000 per day applies at Delhi monuments and Rs 30,000 elsewhere ([ThePrint/PTI](https://theprint.in/india/guidelines-in-place-relating-to-use-of-asi-sites-for-holding-cultural-events-govt/1476626); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/asi-fuming-as-lt-governor-manoj-sinha-holds-prayers-at-martand-sun-temple-393277/amp)). A parliamentary answer notes an exemption for "recognized religious functions" at sites where such functions are recognised, which is the only door through which a wedding at a living shrine inside an ASI zone can pass ([eparlib](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/536201/1/36398.pdf)). The ASI's own stated norm is that no religious prayers are held at protected sites unless they were operational places of worship when the ASI took charge ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/asi-stance-on-religious-practices-at-monuments/print_manually)). No source in this research found a single case of the ASI formally permitting a private wedding at a protected monument in 2024-2026.

Hampi shows what "living temple inside an ASI monument" means in practice. An April 2026 report records the Karnataka endowment department saying weddings at Virupaksha require archaeology-department permission, while the ASI Hampi Circle superintendent says approving weddings is "not its responsibility" and the ASI "has never imposed restrictions on weddings"; the endowment executive officer said "hundreds of weddings used to take place every year but have now become rare", and the Vijayanagara Deputy Commissioner promised a review. What survives is a stripped ritual: priests perform the ceremony on the premises provided there is **no feast, no cooking, no photography and no large gathering** ([Hospet Online](https://hospet.online/?p=18065)). Photography is the second trap. ASI boards at Hampi bar drones; pre-wedding shoots, tripods and commercial photography need special permission ([Hospet Online, 2021](https://hospet.online/strict-regulations-on-photography-at-hampi/)); a 2018 circular required applications at least 15 days ahead, with videography charged at **Rs 50,000 plus a Rs 10,000 deposit** ([Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/karnataka/unless-you-have-permission-you-cant-use-camera-in-hampi)); and a Hampi shoot reportedly needs sign-off from the Hampi World Heritage Area Management Authority, the Deputy Commissioner, the Superintendent of Police and the ASI ([Daijiworld](https://www.daijiworld.com/news/newsDisplay.aspx?newsID=775316)). The ASI's 2017 licensing policy for commercial photographers at monuments was upheld by the Delhi High Court in October 2023 ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-high-court-asi-process-license-photograph-monuments)). A commercial planner does sell "Virupaksha Temple weddings" with a Rs 4,000 "permission coordination" fee, but states no official fee or capacity and its testimonial photos are of Srisailam ([Plannersy](https://plannersy.com/sacred-experiences/virupaksha-temple-wedding-planning/)).

Bhoga Nandeeshwara at Nandi village, the other famous Karnataka "heritage wedding" temple, is in the same position. Weddings happen in the carved stone Vasantha Mantapa inside an ASI-protected complex, with only flowers and fabric allowed and nothing fixed to the stone, reception moved to a nearby venue, and 3-6 months' notice on strong muhurthams ([Panigrahana](https://panigrahana.com/temple-wedding-bangalore.html)); but third-party pages simultaneously say "marriages are not allowed as they are under ASI maintenance" and quote a Rs 1,000 enrolment with priest included ([GoTirupati](https://gotirupati.com/bhoga-nandeeshwara-temple-wedding/)), an older forum answer describes written permission from the ASI Bengaluru Circle plus police and temple permissions with no videography, no decoration, no chairs and no fire except ghee lamps ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.in/FAQ_Answers-g8153697-d3912850-t1366719-What_is_the_procecedure_facilities_and_charges.html)), and a shoot vendor says the temple has been "closed to weddings and pre-wedding shoots" ([1Plus1 Studio](https://www.1plus1studio.com/blog/bhoga-nandeeshwara-temple-couple-shoot)). Availability evidently fluctuates with conservation work. The practical rule for a planner is that any wedding inside an ASI zone must be booked through the temple's endowment office with written clearance from both that office and the ASI circle, budgeted as a short ritual with reception elsewhere, and sold without any promise of professional photography, drones, cooking or pandals.

The firm "no" list on ASI grounds therefore includes Konark (where in January 2025 the Orissa High Court confirmed only ASI-licensed photographers may operate and the ASI closed the Nata Mandap to visitors in November 2025) ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/guides-photographers-at-konark-unesco-site-must-have-asi-licence-hc-1503386366.html); [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/11/09/cal33-od-konark-sun-temple.html)), the Hoysala temples at Belur, Halebidu and Somnathpur (a notification banned "all activities" within 100 m of Somanathapura) ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/around-town/karnatakas-hoysala-era-temples-are-under-pressure-from-unesco-glory-selfie-taking-tourists/1817664)), Ramappa and the Warangal Thousand Pillar temple ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/asi-told-to-set-up-special-development-authority-for-ramappa-temple)), Martand ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/government/jk-lgs-prayers-at-martand-sun-temple-in-violation-of-rules-say-asi-officials)), and the non-living mantapas of Hampi. Thanjavur's Brihadeeswarar is ASI-controlled and no source describes it hosting weddings ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/tamil-nadu/demand-thanjavur-temple-kumbabishekam-tamil-court-tells-tn-govt-respond-116687)); treat Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Darasuram, Mahabalipuram and Lingaraj the same way until a temple office says otherwise.

## Five state regimes decide who signs the permission slip

Outside ASI zones, the body that signs the permission depends entirely on which statute controls the temple, and a planner's counterpart differs by state. In **Tamil Nadu**, HR&CE Act 1959 temples are run by an Executive Officer or Joint Commissioner, and every source describes booking in person at the individual temple's marriage office; no central HR&CE list of halls, tariff or online hall-booking module was found, and the HR&CE e-services cover darshan and seva tickets only ([Prayanamam](https://prayanamam.com/palani-kalyana-mandapam-booking-process-marriage-hall-cost); [Tirthayatra](https://www.tirthayatra.org/?p=38260)). HR&CE policy, as stated by Minister P.K. Sekarbabu, is that "only couples who were Hindus and Indian nationals" may marry in temples, and the department replaced the old VAO-issued "first marriage certificate" with an "unmarried certificate" obtained through TN e-Sevai (service REV-120), alongside address proof, ID and Aadhaar ([The Hindu via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/hr-ce-makes-changes-in-certificates-to-be-submitted-for-weddings-in-temples); [TN e-Sevai](https://tnesevai.tn.gov.in/Default.aspx)). Two 2025 court developments shape supply: the Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court on 19 August 2025 quashed the state's plan to build marriage halls at 27 temples with about Rs 80 crore of temple funds, holding that renting halls is not a "religious purpose" under the HR&CE Act, the Supreme Court declined to stay that order on 16 September 2025, and in June 2026 the government dropped hall projects at 22 temples ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/government-cannot-use-temple-funds-to-build-marriage-halls-madras-hc/); [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/09/16/lgd14-sc-temples-tamil-nadu.html); [The South First, June 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/tamil-nadu-government-drops-temple-marriage-hall-and-commercial-complex-projects-from-assembly-announcements-following-hc-order/)). The existing stock of temple-run halls continues, but it will not grow. Separately, in March 2024 the Madras High Court asked HR&CE to frame an SOP for weddings inside temples, observing that the ceremony can be inside but dining should be elsewhere ([The Hindu via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-hindu-erode-9WW6/20240315/281552295836070)).

**Karnataka**'s Muzrai temples fall under the HRI&CE Act 1997, graded A, B and C by income (B: Rs 5-25 lakh; C: under Rs 5 lakh); A-category temples are handled by the state department and B/C committees by the DC-chaired Zilla Dharmika Parishat ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/panel-formed-fixing-pay-scale-2130197); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Ftemple-committees-will-be-constituted-in-three-months-minister-951060.html)). Fees at Muzrai temples are set by each management committee, not statewide: a 2025 revision at nine temples was decided temple by temple ([Daijiworld](https://daijiworld.com/news/newsDisplay?newsID=1292796)). The ITMS portal covers sevas, donations and rooms, with online choultry booking live at only 12 of 70 temples as of November 2025, and no live kalyana mantapa booking was confirmed ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/pilgrimage-to-get-a-digital-spin-with-online-room-booking-in-karnataka-2696316)). The state's own Saptapadi mass-marriage scheme at 100 A-grade temples, launched in 2020, has struggled with funding ([Business Standard/ANI](https://business-standard.com/article/news-ani/cm-yediyurappa-launches-mass-marriage-program-in-bengaluru-120011000923_1.html); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-government-to-restart-mass-marriage-scheme-1100516.html)). The strongest Karnataka venues are therefore private trusts and mathas rather than Muzrai temples.

**Kerala** splits between the three statutory Devaswom Boards and separately constituted bodies. The Travancore Devaswom Board's online system lists "Puja, Tulabharam, Marriage and Choroon" at 1,250 temples ([Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/news/mobile/news.php?id=1297978&u=); [onlinetdb.com](https://onlinetdb.com/)), and the TDB raised its marriage fee from Rs 750 to Rs 1,100 ([Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/kerala/travancore-devaswom-board-increase-rates-temple-rituals)), but no Board publishes a hall tariff. Guruvayur sits under its own 1978 Act and Managing Committee, and the Kerala High Court in September 2021 affirmed that people have a right to marry there "limited to the infrastructure available", with only flower decoration permitted ([Onmanorama](https://onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2021/09/15/ravi-pillai-son-wedding-guruvayur-kerala-high-court.html)). **Andhra Pradesh** temples sit under the Endowments Act 1987, with TTD as a self-contained system that books its mandapams online and even houses a marriage registrar inside its Kalyana Vedika ([India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/8714/1/act_30_of_1987.pdf); [The Hans India](https://assets.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/tirumala-turns-preferred-wedding-venue-for-new-couples-1040966)). **Telangana**'s Endowments Department offers online arjitha sevas at 38 temples but no online mandapam booking; halls at Yadadri and Bhadrachalam are booked at the temple office ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/tag/online-seva); [GoKshetra](https://gokshetra.com/yadagirigutta-temple-marriage-procedure-online-booking-hall-cost)). In the **north and west**, the big shrines are governed by temple-specific statutes (Kashi Vishwanath Act 1983, Siddhivinayak Act 1980, Shirdi Sansthan Act 2004, Jagannath Temple Act 1954, Nathdwara Act 1959, Mahakaleshwar Adhiniyam 1982) and none of them publishes a public wedding service ([India Code, Kashi](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19610/1/english_29.pdf); [India Code, Siddhivinayak](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/17296/3/shree_siddhi_vinayak.pdf); [India Code, Jagannath](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/5950/1/shri_jagannath_temple_act%2c_1955.pdf)). The exception is Uttarakhand, where the Badrinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee, revived by the 2021 repeal of the Devasthanam Board law, runs the state-promoted wedding regime at Triyuginarayan and Ukhimath ([Uttarakhand HC](https://highcourtofuttarakhand.gov.in/files/Char_Dham_Management_Repeal_Act_2021.pdf); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married)).

Guruvayur's reported online terms (Hindu declaration, 18/21, maximum 20 persons, receipt valid only for the chosen date, cancellation up to one day before with no refund, no extra online charge) come from an unofficial reproduction because the Devaswom site returned errors throughout the research ([guruvayur-info](https://guruvayur-info.pages.dev/marriage-booking); [guruvayurtemple.org](https://guruvayurtemple.org/guruvayur-marriage-booking/)), while the mechanics that matter on the day (tokens at the south-side counter, assembly at Melpathur, 24-person cap, exit via the southern entrance, four permanent mandapams with temporary ones added above 200 bookings, a new permanent mandapam being funded by a Chennai devotee) are newspaper-confirmed ([Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/kerala/general/guruvayur-temple-set-for-record-weddings-in-chingam-1794143); [Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/kerala/general/record-breaking-wedding-bookings-in-guruvayur-as-chingam-arrives-1790527)). The Devaswom's rentable reception venue is Poonthanam Auditorium (full day about Rs 80,000, dining halls Rs 15,000-18,000, kitchen Rs 7,000, ~1,000 capacity, 0487 2556537), while Melpathur is reserved for programmes "offered to the Lord" ([guruvayoorlive.in](https://guruvayoorlive.in/guruvayoor-auditorium)).

Two patterns inside this tier matter for pricing a package. First, across the HR&CE Murugan temples and the AP Endowments temples the temple's own charge is small (a permission or enrolment fee of a few hundred to a few thousand rupees, or a non-AC hall at Rs 2,500-6,000 per day) and everything else (purohit, nadaswaram, garlands, chairs, décor, catering) is explicitly excluded, so the planner's margin and risk sit in vendors, not in the temple fee ([GoTirupati, Tiruchendur](https://gotirupati.com/?p=54480); [GoTirupati, Dwaraka Tirumala](https://gotirupati.com/?p=49042)). Second, the inside-the-complex ceremony is short and quota-limited (Meenakshi 2-3 per day, Kapaleeswarar 1-2, Srirangam's 30-minute thali-tying), while the hall day is what carries the crowd; at Thiruporur the walk-in couples who had not booked were still accommodated at the sanctum with a garland exchange, which shows the "quota" is enforced loosely on peak days but cannot be relied on for a paying client ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/crowd-surge-at-tiruporur-temple-with-over-100-weddings)).

## Tier 3: conditional venues to sell only after a written yes

A third group can host a wedding in principle, but a planner should treat each as a bespoke enquiry rather than a listed product, because permission is discretionary, the venue is in an ASI zone, or the only evidence is a single unverified page. Hampi's Virupaksha and Bhoga Nandeeshwara, discussed above, head this list. Somnath's trust FAQ confirms a banquet/conference hall (02876-233533) and a bookable Yagna Shala, but never mentions marriages, bans photography and prescribes modest dress, so a Hindu rite there is plausible only after clearance from the general manager ([somnath.org](https://somnath.org/faq)). Delhi's Chhatarpur Mandir uses its Markande Hall for weddings "sometimes", and "only when organized in conjunction with the temple" ([Explurger](https://www.explurger.com/pd/ztg6git86s7qfkdfi)); Amritsar's Durgiana committee mentions a big community hall attached to the Shiv Ganga Mandir with no rental information ([durgianamandir.in](https://durgianamandir.in/)); Shimla's Ram Mandir (Sood Sabha) rents its grounds hall with a ban on meat, fish and alcohol, and after an April 2026 row over a nikah booking ruled that non-Hindu bookings may hold a reception only, with a written guarantee of no religious activity ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/row-erupts-in-shimla-over-proposed-nikah-at-ram-mandir-hall--20260407152150)). Kolhapur Mahalakshmi's "function hall" and Jejuri's "Lagna Pooja" at Rs 1,000-1,500 rest solely on SEO pages ([gokshetra](https://gokshetra.com/?p=1604); [gokshetra](https://gokshetra.com/khandoba-temple-marriage-procedure-registration-online-booking)). In Tamil Nadu, Tiruvannamalai Arunachaleswarar has "no current human-wedding venue" per one guide and a "designated area on the premises" per another, so it needs administration permission case by case ([GoKshetra](https://gokshetra.com/tiruvannamalai-temple-marriage-procedure-rules-registration/)), and Chidambaram Nataraja, run by the hereditary Podhu Dikshitars rather than HR&CE, holds weddings only in the Pandiyanayakar Sannathi; in 2019 two priests were suspended for solemnising a wedding in the Thousand-pillared hall, which is reserved for festivals ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/190919/podhu-dikshithars-admit-to-committing-lapse-in-permitting-marria.html)). Kanipakam's official booking menu carries no wedding product, so weddings there are in private halls ([GoKshetra](https://gokshetra.com/kanipakam-temple-free-marriage-procedure-hall-booking-cost)). In Haridwar and Rishikesh the realistic venues are ashrams, not temples: Parmarth Niketan regularly hosts "sacred weddings" on its own Ganga ghat with no published fee ([sadhviji.org](https://www.sadhviji.org/seva/parmarth)), while Har Ki Pauri is controlled by the Ganga Sabha and since January 2026 carries "non-Hindu prohibited" signs with no wedding permit route ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/in-haridwar-posters-barring-non-hindus-from-entry-come-up-at-har-ki-pauri--20260116194204)).

## Tier 4: temples a planner should list as darshan-only

A website gains credibility by saying plainly where weddings are not available, because the most-searched temple names are mostly in this group. Inside the Tirumala Srivari temple no private wedding is performed; TTD's own arrangement is the Kalyana Vedika and Purohit Sangham mandapam on the hill, and guides uniformly state "no marriages are allowed before the main deity or inside the temple" ([GoTirupati](https://gotirupati.com/?p=68697); [The Hans India](https://assets.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/tirumala-turns-preferred-wedding-venue-for-new-couples-1040966)). Puri Jagannath hosts only deity weddings (Rukmini Harana at the Vivaha Mandap, June 2026) and bars non-Hindus at the Lion's Gate ([FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/puri-jagannath-temple-closes-darshan-temporarily-for-rukmini-harana-and-sacred-wedding-rituals); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/885465/scroll_in)); Lingaraj likewise stages only the deity's Sital Sasthi wedding ([OdishaTV](https://odishatv.in/news/odisha/lord-lingaraj-s-divine-wedding-to-be-held-on-may-30-confirms-temple-administration-263879)). Kashi Vishwanath and the Ayodhya Ram Mandir publish no marriage service and 2024-26 coverage records none ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashi_Vishwanath); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/news-brief/ram-mandirs-dhwaja-hoisting-on-vivah-panchami-president-murmu-to-be-present-at-november-25-event-marking-temples-completion)). At Banke Bihari, Vrindavan, a couple's ring exchange in the VIP gallery in September 2026 became a security scandal ([UNI](https://www.uniindia.com/north-india/religion-up-banke-bihari-couple/191810)). Ujjain's own tourism planner states "ceremonies cannot take place directly inside the main sanctum of Mahakaleshwar Temple" ([wedding.ujjainmahakaltourism.com](https://wedding.ujjainmahakaltourism.com)); the MP Chief Minister's son married in a 22-couple mass wedding on the Kshipra bank at Sanwarkhedi, not in the temple ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/madhya-pradesh/madhya-pradesh-cm-mohan-yadavs-son-abhimanyu-yadav-ties-knot-in-mass-wedding-in-ujjain-2025-11-30-1019627)). Pushkar's Brahma temple bars married men from the sanctum ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Temple,_Pushkar)). Vaishno Devi's Shrine Board lists accommodation and cloak rooms only ([SMVDSB](https://legacy.maavaishnodevi.org/katra-town-facilities.aspx)). Siddhivinayak, Shirdi (whose "halls" are dormitory accommodation and which requires newly married couples to show a registration certificate to share a room), Trimbakeshwar, Nathdwara, Dwarkadhish, Ambaji, Dakor, Khatu Shyam, Salasar, Tuljapur and Dagdusheth produced no evidence of any wedding facility; Pandharpur institutionalised the alternative by introducing VIP darshan for newlyweds from January 2025 ([sai.org.in](https://sai.org.in/en/node/26); [The Bridge Chronicle](https://www.thebridgechronicle.com/news/pandharpur-vitthal-rukmini-temple-offers-vip-darshan-for-newlywed-couples)).

Several temples have shut or never opened the door for stated reasons that a planner should know. Bengaluru's Halasuru Someshwara temple, which once did 100-150 weddings a year, stopped 6-7 years ago because priests were repeatedly summoned in divorce cases and eloping couples used fake documents ([Hinduism Today/Mathrubhumi](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/2025/12/25/no-more-weddings-here-bengaluru-temple-bans-marriages-over-divorce-troubles/)). Srivilliputhur Andal "does not own any facilities that would allow it to preside over marriage ceremonies" ([Prayanamam](https://prayanamam.com/?p=6961)). Cheranallur Karthyayani (Cochin Devaswom Board) holds no marriage ceremony at all because of the goddess's interrupted-wedding legend ([Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/lifestyle/general/marriage-stopped-because-bride-became-menstruous-there-is-a-temple-in-kerala-where-fragrance-and-red-flowers-are-not-allowed-1627056)). Kachamkurissi (Malabar Devaswom Board) does not "entertain love marriages" without parental consent ([Outlook](https://outlookindia.com/national/transgender-couple-barred-from-tying-the-knot-at-temple-news-239798)). Sabarimala, Padmanabhaswamy (Hindus only, churidar-clad women turned away) and Vadakkunnathan have no documented wedding practice ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/churidar-clad-women-denied-entry-padmanabhaswamy-temple.html)). Puducherry's Manakula Vinayagar shows only darshan and seva bookings ([Dinamalar](https://temple.dinamalar.com/en/new_en.php?id=620)). For the famous CDB and TDB temples of Kerala (Chottanikkara, Ettumanoor, Vaikom, Thrikkakara, Kodungallur, Mannarasala) and for a long list of Karnataka, Rajasthan, Bihar and Jharkhand shrines, the research found nothing either way; those are gaps to fill by phone, not confirmed refusals.

## Hindus only, parents present, saree and veshti: the rules converge everywhere

Eligibility is the condition most likely to sink a booking, and it is near-uniform. Tamil Nadu's minister limits temple weddings to Hindu Indian nationals, and an HR&CE executive officer at Chennai's Thiruvetteeswarar temple cancelled a scheduled Christian wedding in May 2023 with a circular stating "Only Hindus will be allowed to marry in this temple" ([Organiser](https://organiser.org/2023/05/10/173380/bharat/tamil-nadu-following-protests-wedding-of-christian-couple-scheduled-to-take-place-at-temple-gets-cancelled/)). Guruvayur requires a declaration that the parties are Hindu and has demanded an Arya Samajam certificate before admitting converts ([guruvayur-info](https://guruvayur-info.pages.dev/marriage-booking); [Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/web-news/en/2020/05/NMAN0146632/1.html)); TTD, Dharmasthala, Attukal and every AP, Telangana and Karnataka venue say the same. Beyond faith, the de facto rule is an arranged first marriage with both sets of parents present: TTD bars "love marriages and second marriages", Dharmasthala bars second marriages, HR&CE temples require parents' Aadhaar and a parent's signature in the register, and Thiru-Kachamkurissi refused a couple on the ground that it does not host love marriages ([The Hans India](https://assets.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/tirumala-turns-preferred-wedding-venue-for-new-couples-1040966); [GoTirupati, Dharmasthala](https://gotirupati.com/dharmasthala-marriage-hall-price-online-booking-procedure-and-contact/); [Prayanamam, Tiruchendur](https://prayanamam.com/?p=2425)). Inter-caste arranged marriages are accepted at most TN and Karnataka venues with a police NOC and notarised parental consent, but Devanathaswamy (Thiruvanthipuram) reportedly allows same-community marriages only, Srisailam's community halls require both parties from the same community, and the Guwahati temples refuse inter-caste couples outright ([GoTirupati, Devanathaswamy](https://gotirupati.com/?p=63678); [GoTirupati, Srisailam](https://gotirupati.com/?p=50935); [NE Now](https://m.nenow.in/article/assam/assam-guwahati-couple-denied-temple-wedding-due-to-inter-caste-marriage/410540)). Statutory ages of 18 for the bride and 21 for the groom are enforced at every counter, Odisha's Endowments Commission ordered age-document checks at all its shrines in 2021, and Chidambaram Dikshitars were arrested over child-marriage complaints ([OdishaBytes](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-temples-barred-from-solemnising-child-marriages/amp); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/police-arrest-chidambaram-nataraja-temple-dikshitars-over-complaints-of-child-marriage-several-such-incidents-suspected/)). The planner's intake form should therefore capture, for every couple: Aadhaar for both and all four parents, age proof (birth certificate, SSLC/10th certificate, passport), an unmarried certificate or affidavit, the wedding invitation, passport photos, parental consent, and ID for two or three witnesses; that one dossier satisfies every counter reviewed and is reused at civil registration.

Mixed-faith guest lists are a second constraint. Since the Madras High Court's Palani order of 30 January 2024, Tamil Nadu must display boards stating that non-Hindus are not allowed beyond the kodimaram, admitting a non-Hindu only on a written undertaking of faith recorded in a temple register ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/01/31/palani-temple-dispute-non-hindus-cannot-allowed-inside-temple-without-written-undertaking-madras-high-court/)); at Guruvayur, Puri and Padmanabhaswamy non-Hindu guests cannot enter at all ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/news/amid-jagans-tirupati-row-here-are-9-major-indian-temples-that-bar-entry-of-non-hindus-147341)). Dress is temple-specific rather than statutory: the Madras High Court's 2015 statewide dress code was set aside in April 2016, leaving each temple to frame its own under the Temple Entry Authorisation Act 1947 ([LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/madras-high-court-quashes-dress-code-temple-entry-2)), and in practice the groom wears a dhoti/veshti (shirt allowed at Guruvayur, bare-chested for ordinary darshan) and the bride a saree, with footwear removed and no Western attire at Parthasarathy or shorts and sleeveless tops at Meenakshi ([Kerala Tourism](https://www.keralatourism.org/faq/is-there-any-dress-code-for-guruvayur-temple); [GoKshetra, Meenakshi](https://gokshetra.com/meenakshi-amman-temple-marriage-guide/)).

Photography, food, sound and décor are where a planner's promises most often collide with temple rules. Guruvayur sells a separate photo/video ticket, caps crews at two photographers and two videographers, and bars photography on the eastern nadappanthal after the ceremony ([guruvayurtemple.org](https://guruvayurtemple.org/guruvayur-marriage-booking/); [Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/kerala/general/record-marriages-in-guruvayur-ambalanadayil-354-weddings-solemnized-1380258)); Meenakshi and Parthasarathy require authorised photographers and ban drones and tripods; Somnath bans photography outright; Udupi's Paryaya matha banned pre- and post-wedding shoots on the Rathabeedi in April 2025 ([The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/11/mes7-ka-temple-photoshoots.html)); and at any ASI site a shoot is commercial filming requiring 15 days' notice and a fee. Food inside the complex is almost never allowed: Vadapalani bars outside food, Kapaleeswarar bars food inside the temple, Hampi bars cooking, Bhoga Nandeeshwara sends meals to the Samudaya Bhavan, the Madras High Court wants dining outside, and ISKCON venues allow only in-house sattvik catering; alcohol is banned at every trust hall found (ISKCON Dwarka, Fatorpa, Shimla Ram Mandir, Kukke) and Guruvayur's decision to open its Oottupura dining hall to non-Hindus was revoked ([ISKCON Bangalore](https://www.iskconbangalore.org/kalyana-mantapa/); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/guruvayur-temple-revokes-decision-allow-non-hindus-feast-hall-consult-god-80129)). Sound follows the same line: TTD bans DJ music in all its mandapams, BKTC bans DJs, brass bands and firecrackers at Triyuginarayan, Parthasarathy bars loud music, Attukal asks for none, and only ISKCON Dwarka explicitly allows a DJ ([GoTirupati, TTD](https://gotirupati.com/?p=62368); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/reports/uttarakhand-has-a-trump-card-for-attracting-destination-weddings-the-temple-where-shiva-and-parvati-got-married)). Décor at the ceremony point is flowers only at Guruvayur, flowers and fabric with nothing fixed to stone at Bhoga Nandeeshwara, and "no decoration" at several HR&CE temples. Timing windows are tight and temple-specific: Guruvayur 4-5 am to 12.45 pm with evening slots, Meenakshi after 6 am in 5-10 am, 10-12 and 4-7 pm slots, Parthasarathy 6-12 and 4-8, Triyuginarayan's BKTC "window" of November-April against planners' May-October advice (a conflict the planner should resolve with BKTC directly), and hall allotments that run 24 hours from 2, 3 or 4 pm.

## Registration and permits turn a ritual into a marriage

A temple ceremony produces a valid Hindu marriage only if it is solemnised with the customary rites under section 7 of the Hindu Marriage Act (complete on the seventh step where saptapadi is part of the rite), and it becomes provable only through registration under state rules made under section 8 or a state compulsory-registration law; section 8(5) says omission to register does not affect validity, but the Supreme Court in Dolly Rani (2024) and the Allahabad and Gujarat High Courts have held that a registration certificate confers nothing where the ceremonies were not actually performed ([devgan.in](https://devgan.in/hma/chapter_02.php); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1067352/rites-and-ceremonies-must-be-performed-for-hindu-marriage-to-be-valid-says-supreme-court); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/hindu-marriage-void-despite-valid-certificate-if-saptapadi-not-performed-gujarat-high-court)). Every temple document in this report (TTD's "ceremonial" certificate, the Guruvayur receipt that "is not a legal document", HR&CE receipts, the Yadadri hall receipt, BKTC's certificate) is evidence of solemnisation, not a marriage certificate ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/news/planning-to-get-married-in-tirumala-heres-how-to-register-197575); [SacredYatra](https://tours.sacredyatra.com/?p=4950)). The state windows differ: Kerala's Common Rules 2008 require a memorandum within 45 days with a copy of the religious authority's certificate and two witnesses, with fines after 45 days and Registrar General permission after a year ([LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/the-kerala-registration-of-marriages-common-rules-2008/DFDB)); Tamil Nadu's 2009 Act makes registration compulsory for all marriages, with the priest a signatory to the memorandum and a 90-day window cited by guides but not verified against the Act ([Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f11969fca195c2c0cee14); [GoKshetra](https://gokshetra.com/tiruvannamalai-temple-marriage-procedure-rules-registration/)); Karnataka registers through Kaveri 2.0 with three witnesses ([Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/karnataka-launches-project-to-obtain-marriage-certificate-over-kaveri-2-0/amp)); Uttarakhand and Rajasthan have compulsory-registration Acts and Uttarakhand an online e-marriage portal ([Uttarakhand HC](https://highcourtofuttarakhand.gov.in/files/Compulsory_Registration_of_Marriage_Act_2010.pdf); [Rajasthan Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22441/1/rajasthan_compulsory_registration_of_marriages_act_in_english.pdf)). TTD is uniquely convenient, with a Hindu Marriage Registrar at Room 233, SMC Guest House Complex, Tirumala, open 10:30-17:00, needing age and residence proof, a wedding photo, the mandapam receipt, the invitation and three witnesses ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/news/planning-to-get-married-in-tirumala-heres-how-to-register-197575)); Guruvayur has a registration centre at the municipality ([guruvayurtemple.org](https://guruvayurtemple.org/?p=15253)). Given the case law, a planner should treat photographs of the saptapadi and thali-tying as legal evidence, not just keepsakes, and should note that the NRI 30-day registration rule exists only in an un-enacted 2019 Bill ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-registration-of-marriage-of-non-resident-indian-bill-2019)).

Event permits around the temple are governed by national rules that bite hardest near heritage sites. Loudspeakers need written permission under Rule 5 of the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules 2000 and are barred from 10 pm to 6 am except in closed halls, with the state able to extend to midnight on up to 15 festival days a year ([elaw.in](https://elaw.in/noise/rules.html)); ASI conditions independently stop music at 10 pm. Drones fall under the Drone Rules 2021 green/yellow/red zoning on the Digital Sky platform, with yellow/red-zone flying without permission a cognisable offence, and ASI signage at Hampi bars drones outright ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/aviation/1108844/the-drone-rules-2021-giving-wings-to-the-drone-industry-in-india); [Hospet Online](https://hospet.online/strict-regulations-on-photography-at-hampi/)). Police intimation for baraat processions, fire-safety norms for homa inside halls, FSSAI obligations for caterers and municipal pandal permits were not covered by any source found and remain open items for the planner's own compliance checklist.

## Conclusion

The map that emerges inverts the intuition behind most "temple wedding" marketing. The temples with the greatest name recognition in the north and west are the least usable, because their statutes and crowd management leave no room for private rites, while the usable venues are the institutions that have industrialised the wedding as a seva: Guruvayur's six-mandapam, token-driven morning; TTD's free Vedika with a registrar in the building and a portal-booked network of town halls; HR&CE Murugan temples that process a hundred couples on a muhurtham day; and the Uttarakhand committee that wrote a Shantikunj-style rulebook because the state wants destination weddings. A planner's product is therefore not "a wedding at a famous temple" but a ten-to-thirty-minute, Hindu-only, parent-attended rite at a sanctioned mandapam, wrapped around a feast and reception that the temple itself will not allow inside, plus a civil registration the temple will not perform. The 2025 Madras High Court ruling against temple-funded halls, the ASI's exclusion of private applicants, and Halasuru's withdrawal after being dragged into divorce litigation all point the same way: supply of temple-run wedding infrastructure is static or shrinking, which makes early booking windows (TTD's 90 days, Guruvayur's 30, HR&CE's two to three months, Triyuginarayan's three to six for peak muhurats) a real commercial constraint rather than a formality.

For the website, the evidence supports a three-label system rather than a single list: "bookable" for the Tier 1 and 2 venues, with rupee figures shown only where an official or newspaper source exists and otherwise replaced by "confirm with temple office"; "on request" for ASI-zone and discretionary venues such as Virupaksha, Bhoga Nandeeshwara, Somnath and Chhatarpur, sold only with written clearance in hand; and "darshan only" for the famous shrines, framed as a pre- or post-wedding blessing package. Two gaps are worth closing before launch by direct enquiry: the live tariffs on the TTD and Guruvayur portals, which the research could not read, and the Kerala Devaswom Board, Cochin and Malabar temples, where the English-language web is silent but the TDB's online "Marriage" vazhipadu suggests a large, undocumented inventory of modest, deliverable venues.
