# Legal and administrative framework for weddings at Hindu temples in India (2025-2026), from a wedding planner's permissions perspective

Scope note: India only. Research conducted 30 Oct 2026. Statutory texts cited by section where a primary or near-primary source was reachable; where a figure or rule comes from a news report or third-party guide rather than the statute/official portal, that is flagged. Several official portals (hrce.tn.gov.in, cr.lsgkerala.gov.in, kygcms.tn.gov.in, indiacode PDFs) returned 403/503 during this research, so some items rest on secondary reporting and are listed as gaps.

## Key Question 1: ASI, the AMASR Act 1958 (as amended 2010), and events/ceremonies/photography at centrally protected monuments; Hampi/HWHAMA

### Takeaway
ASI's standing guidelines (confirmed by the Culture Minister in Parliament, March 2023) make private functions at centrally protected monuments effectively unavailable: only government departments/public bodies may apply, only "classical" cultural events qualify, and "commercial or religious activities" are expressly prohibited — so a wedding ceremony cannot be permitted on ASI-protected ground under those guidelines. The practical route at "living" temples inside ASI areas (e.g., Virupaksha at Hampi) is that the endowment department/temple runs the ritual, while ASI regulates cooking, photography, structures and crowds; reports from Hampi (April 2026) show the two departments disagreeing about who decides, which has made weddings there rare. Professional photography/videography and drones at ASI sites need advance ASI permission and fees.

### Cited Findings
- Under Rule 7(1) of the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Rules, 1959, "meetings, receptions, parties, entertainment or conferences" cannot be held at a protected monument without written permission from the Central Government (reported in context of ASI objecting to the J&K Lt Governor holding prayers at the ASI-protected Martand Sun Temple). — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/asi-fuming-as-lt-governor-manoj-sinha-holds-prayers-at-martand-sun-temple-393277/amp); [The Wire](https://thewire.in/government/jk-lgs-prayers-at-martand-sun-temple-in-violation-of-rules-say-asi-officials)
- Rule 8 of the 1959 Rules: no person shall, within a protected monument, do any act which causes or is likely to cause damage to any part of the monument, or discharge fire-arms (cited in a Lok Sabha document). — [eparlib.nic.in](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/646474/1/145780.pdf)
- ASI guidelines for events at monuments (as stated by Union Culture Minister G. Kishan Reddy in a written Lok Sabha reply, PTI report dated 27 March 2023): ASI "generally does not permit monuments, or land attached to them, to host functions or events"; circles may permit use "only exceptionally" if the DG ASI or Superintending Archaeologist is satisfied there will be no damage; only monuments specifically identified for cultural events are eligible; events must be "of a high standard, preferably classical" (classical music, dance, drama); **prohibited: events with ticketed/paid admission, "commercial or religious activities", any construction except a movable temporary stage with no masonry**; **only government departments and public bodies may be granted permission; private individuals, private bodies and commercial organisations are excluded**; permission is normally for one day; temporary structures must be erected and removed the same day by 11 pm; functions must end by 10 pm with no music/loudspeakers after that; ASI may impose conditions on parking, visitor numbers, noise and light; refundable security deposit Rs 50,000/day for Delhi monuments and Rs 30,000/day in other circles; violations lead to a police complaint. — [ThePrint (PTI)](https://theprint.in/india/guidelines-in-place-relating-to-use-of-asi-sites-for-holding-cultural-events-govt/1476626); same report via [OrissaPOST](https://www.orissapost.com/guidelines-in-place-relating-to-use-of-asi-sites-for-holding-cultural-events-govt)
- Per ASI rules, "no religious rituals are permitted at non-living monuments"; a Parliamentary Standing Committee recommended allowing prayers/rituals at protected sites of religious significance (secondary summary). — [Prepp summary of Standing Committee report](https://prepp.in/news/e-492-house-panel-recommends-prayers-rituals-at-protected-sites-of-religious-significance-upsc-current-affairs); [PRS summary](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/creation-of-regulatory-framework-for-protection-of-historical-sites-and-monuments)
- AMASR (Amendment and Validation) Act 2010: area within 100 m of a protected monument is the "prohibited area" and a further belt (Wikipedia gives conflicting 200 m / 300 m figures in different sections) is the "regulated area"; the National Monuments Authority (est. 2011) regulates construction/repairs in these zones. The culture minister indicated in 2023 that further amendments were expected. — [Wikipedia, AMASR Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Monuments_and_Archaeological_Sites_and_Remains_Act,_1958) (section numbers not given on this page; see Gaps)
- Hampi weddings (article dated 21 April 2026): the Karnataka endowment department says weddings at Hampi's ancient temples require permission from the archaeology department, while ASI's Hampi Circle superintendent Ramakrishna Reddy says approving or rejecting weddings "is not its responsibility", that ASI "has never imposed restrictions on weddings" and has no objection to rule-compliant weddings near the temple; endowment EO Anjaneyalu said "hundreds of weddings used to take place every year but have now become rare due to the rules laid down by the ASI"; cooking and photography within temple premises are restricted; simple weddings without feasts, photography or large gatherings continue with priests performing rituals; Vijayanagara DC Kavita S. Mannikeri said she would "review how weddings were conducted in the past". Many families now opt for private halls. — [Hospet Online](https://hospet.online/?p=18065)
- Hampi photography circular (ASI, reported 31 March 2018): photo shoots at Hampi and other protected sites need advance permission "at least 15 days before"; monopods/flash need prior permission and a fee; mobile phone photos are free but cannot be used commercially; selfie sticks prohibited; commercial photo use fee Rs 750; videography Rs 50,000 plus Rs 10,000 refundable deposit. — [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/karnataka/unless-you-have-permission-you-cant-use-camera-in-hampi)
- Hampi (Jan 2021, after a pre-wedding shoot controversy inside the Vijaya Vittala temple): ASI boards state drone photography is not permitted; pre-wedding photoshoots, tripods, commercial and high-resolution photography, drawings and sketches require special permission (ASI Deputy Superintendent P. Kalimuthu). — [Hospet Online](https://hospet.online/strict-regulations-on-photography-at-hampi/)
- A 2020 report on a viral pre-wedding video at Hampi said a photoshoot needs sign-off from several bodies including the Hampi World Heritage Area Management Authority, the Deputy Commissioner, the Superintendent of Police and ASI; a probe was ordered. — [Udayavani](https://udayavani.com/english-news/viral-video-probe-ordered-into-pre-wedding-photoshoot-at-hampi-heritage-site) (page returned 403 on fetch; details from search snippet)
- Deccan Herald on a film crew shooting near the Elephant Stables at Hampi posing as tourists: fee for commercial shooting at local monuments was raised from Rs 10,000 to Rs 1 lakh from 1 April (year not visible). — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/row-over-video-shoot-near-2068922)
- Hampi World Heritage Area Management Authority Act, 2002 (Karnataka Act 18 of 2003): prior permission of the Authority is needed for development in the heritage area; s.16 allows the Authority to charge a fee for permissions under s.14. — [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/7520/1/18_of_2003_%28e%29.pdf); [PRS copy](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/karnataka/2003/2003KR18.pdf)
- ASI photography policy generally: in July 2018 ASI allowed photography inside all protected monuments except the Taj Mahal mausoleum, Ajanta Caves and Leh Palace; professional photographers, historians and academics still require permission under a 2016 order. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/nation/asi-permits-cameras-in-all-but-3-monuments-619732); [Business Standard (PTI)](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/asi-orders-photography-within-monument-premises-118071201376_1.html)
- ASI's 24 May 2017 policy mandating licences for (commercial) photographers working at ASI monuments was upheld by the Delhi High Court (Oct 2023). — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-high-court-asi-process-license-photograph-monuments); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/delhi-hc-upholds-licensing-policy-for-photographers-at-asi-protected-monuments--20231012125705)
- Example of ASI event controversy: ASI Nagpur Circle criticised over a "Deepotsav" event at Paoni Fort (Oct 2022), with the report asserting ASI does not allow such events at centrally protected monuments. — [The Hitavada](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2022/10/30/ASI-Nagpur-Circle-lands-in-fresh-controversy-over-Deepotsav-event-at-Paoni-Fort.html)
- Incredible India's listing for Hampi's Virupaksha temple states "simple marriage will be done as per rules" (no rules specified). — [Incredible India](https://prod.incredibleindia.gov.in/content/incredible-india-v2/en/destinations/hampi/virupaksha-temple.html)

### Inferences
- Because ASI's event guidelines exclude private applicants and religious activity, a planner should not expect any ASI "wedding permission" as such; the realistic model at living temples inside ASI zones (Virupaksha/Hampi; similarly Brihadeeswarar/Thanjavur, Ekambareswarar etc.) is a temple-run ritual booked through the endowment/temple office, with the planner complying with ASI prohibitions on structures, cooking, loudspeakers, professional photography/drones. Expect no pandal, no feast and no commercial shoot inside the protected area.
- The 2026 Hampi dispute shows the de facto gatekeeper is the local endowment EO plus the district administration, with ASI holding a veto via its rules on photography/cooking/structures; written clearance from both is prudent.
- Pre-wedding/wedding shoots at ASI sites are treated as commercial filming: budget for the videography fee (Rs 50,000 + deposit reported in 2018 at Hampi; Rs 1 lakh for film shoots per Deccan Herald) and apply 15+ days ahead.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the current consolidated text of the AMASR Act or the 1959 Rules (asi.nic.in PDF / indiacode) to quote exact section/rule numbers for prohibited/regulated areas, offences (s.30) and the rules on photography/filming (Rule 7, Rule 8, Rule 9?) — rule numbers above are from secondary reports.
- No published official ASI order specifically on weddings; no confirmed case of ASI formally "permitting" a wedding at a protected monument found. Published cases are of refusals/probes for shoots and of religious events drawing ASI objections (Martand).
- Could not confirm whether Brihadeeswarar (Thanjavur) or Ekambareswarar have any specific wedding arrangements; no reliable source found.
- The ASI list of monuments identified for cultural events was not reproduced in any source found.
- HWHAMA's own permission procedure/fees for events or shoots not found on an official page.

## Key Question 2: State endowment regimes and how they handle wedding bookings at temples

### Takeaway
In the southern states the temple-controlling statute determines the booking counter: Karnataka (HRI&CE Act 1997; Muzrai; A/B/C categories by income; ITMS portal), Tamil Nadu (HR&CE Act 1959; temple-by-temple hall bookings; HR&CE-regulated document rules), Kerala (Travancore, Cochin, Malabar Devaswom Boards; Guruvayur under its own 1978 Act with a dedicated marriage ticket system), Andhra Pradesh (Endowments Act 1987; TTD's free Kalyana Mandapam with online booking 90 days ahead), Telangana (Endowments; mostly offline temple-office bookings). Northern/western shrines (Kashi Vishwanath, Siddhivinayak, Shirdi, Jagannath Puri) are run under temple-specific Acts and none of the sources located show a general public wedding-booking facility.

### Inferences

## Key Question 3: Written applications, typical documents, how far ahead bookings open, cancellation/refund

### Takeaway
Across the big southern temples the recurring document set is: Aadhaar of bride, groom and both sets of parents; age proof (birth certificate/SSLC/10th certificate/passport); proof of marital status (unmarried certificate/affidavit in TN); wedding invitation; passport photos; parents present or consenting. Horoscopes are not required anywhere in the sources; community/caste certificate appears only in some TN temple guides. TTD booking opens 90 days out; Guruvayur tickets up to ~30 days out; TN guides advise 2-3 months. No official cancellation/refund rules were located.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A planner should assemble a single dossier per couple (Aadhaar x6, age proofs, invitation, photos, marital-status affidavit, parents' consent letter) because every temple-run counter reviewed asks for some subset of these and the same set is reused for civil registration.
- Because TTD, Guruvayur and Yadadri all state "Hindus only" and TTD bars second marriages, planners should confirm eligibility before paying a booking fee.

## Key Question 4: Entry restrictions affecting a planner — non-Hindu entry, dress codes, photography/drones, sound, caste/community restrictions and legal challenges

### Takeaway
Non-Hindu entry is barred at Guruvayur, Puri Jagannath and Padmanabhaswamy, and since the Madras HC's Palani order (30 Jan 2024) Tamil Nadu HR&CE temples must display boards barring non-Hindus beyond the kodimaram unless they give a written undertaking of faith — a direct constraint on mixed-faith guest lists. Tamil Nadu's court-ordered dress code (Nov 2015) was set aside in April 2016, leaving dress codes to each temple under the Temple Entry Authorisation Act 1947; Guruvayur enforces dhoti/saree. Photography and drones are restricted at ASI sites and temple-specific photo tickets exist (Guruvayur).

### Cited Findings
- Madras HC, D. Senthilkumar v. Government of Tamil Nadu, 2024 SCC OnLine Mad 154 (S. Srimathy J., Madurai Bench, 30 Jan 2024): State must install boards at temple entrances, near the kodimaram and prominent places stating "non-Hindus are not allowed inside temple after Kodimaram"; a non-Hindu who wishes to visit a deity may be admitted only after a written undertaking of faith and adherence to Hindu/temple customs, recorded in a temple register; relied on the Tamil Nadu Temple Entry Authorisation Act, 1947, the HR&CE Act (definition of "temple"; s.10 requiring department employees to be Hindu), Arts. 13, 15, 25-28; noted Rule 4-A of the Temple Entry Rules permitting non-Hindu entry was struck down in Kalyan Dass v. State of TN (1972); the SCC Online report says the directions were addressed to "all Hindu Temples" though media reports differ on scope (Palani-only vs statewide). — [SCC Online Blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/01/31/palani-temple-dispute-non-hindus-cannot-allowed-inside-temple-without-written-undertaking-madras-high-court/); [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/hc-directs-tn-government-to-put-up-boards-restricting-non-hindus-till-flagpole-in-temples-764526); [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/madras-high-court-directs-restorationinstallation-of-boards-barring-non-hindus-in-temples-1518103)
- Puri Jagannath: signboard at the Lion's Gate says only orthodox Hindus may enter; the administration treats this as integral to tradition; in 2018 the Supreme Court asked the management whether non-Hindus could be allowed subject to dress code and traditions; VHP opposed. — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/885465/scroll_in); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/archives/vhp-opposes-entry-of-non-hindus-at-jagannath-temple-679661.html)
- Guruvayur enforces the non-Hindu bar strictly (singer Yesudas famously barred); Padmanabhaswamy bars non-Hindus, reportedly allowing entry on a signed affidavit of belief in Hinduism to the EO. — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/news/amid-jagans-tirupati-row-here-are-9-major-indian-temples-that-bar-entry-of-non-hindus-147341); [Deccan Herald editorial](https://www.deccanherald.com/opinion/editorial/temples-must-be-open-all-680128.html)

- With no statewide dress code in force in Tamil Nadu, the operative rule is each temple's own (often dhoti/saree for the mandapam); Kerala temples (TDB/Guruvayur) enforce mundu/saree in practice.

## Key Question 5: Legal registration — HMA ss.7/8, state rules, temple certificates, documents to collect, NRI requirements

### Takeaway
A temple wedding becomes a legally provable marriage only through (a) valid solemnisation under HMA s.7 (customary rites; saptapadi where applicable) and (b) registration under the state rules made under HMA s.8 or a state compulsory-registration law (Tamil Nadu 2009 Act; Kerala Common Rules 2008; Karnataka 1976 Act). Temple certificates (TTD, Guruvayur, HR&CE temples) are ceremonial/evidence of solemnisation that registrars accept as supporting documents, not legal certificates; courts (SC 2024, Allahabad, Gujarat, Patna HCs) have held that even a registration certificate does not validate a marriage if the ceremonies were not performed. Kerala requires filing within 45 days with the religious authority's certificate and two witnesses. The NRI 30-day registration requirement exists only in the un-enacted 2019 Bill.

### Cited Findings
- HMA 1955 s.5 (conditions: no living spouse; groom 21/bride 18; mental capacity; prohibited degrees/sapinda unless custom permits); s.7(1) "A Hindu marriage may be solemnized in accordance with the customary rites and ceremonies of either party thereto"; s.7(2) where rites include saptapadi the marriage is complete on the seventh step; s.8(1) State Government may make rules for a Hindu Marriage Register "for the purpose of facilitating the proof of Hindu marriages"; s.8(2) State may make entry compulsory with fine up to Rs 25; s.8(4) register entries admissible as evidence; s.8(5) "the validity of any Hindu marriage shall in no way be affected by the omission to make the entry". — [devgan.in HMA Chapter II](https://devgan.in/hma/chapter_02.php)
- Supreme Court (Dolly Rani v. Manish Kumar Chanchal, 2024): a Hindu marriage must be solemnised with the requisite ceremonies; registration under s.8 facilitates proof but does not confer legitimacy where no valid ceremony took place. — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1067352/rites-and-ceremonies-must-be-performed-for-hindu-marriage-to-be-valid-says-supreme-court); [SCC Online blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=321332)
- Allahabad HC: an Arya Samaj Mandir marriage certificate is not proof of marriage; saptapadi essential; a registration not made under s.8 and state rules is not evidence of a Hindu marriage. — [LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/high-court/allahabad-high-court/allahabad-high-court-rules-arya-samaj-mandir-marriage-certificate-not-proof-of-marriage-saptapadi-ceremony-essential-263364)
- Gujarat HC: marriage void despite valid certificate if saptapadi not performed; Patna HC (reported Aug 2026): voter-list entry alone not conclusive proof of Hindu marriage. — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/hindu-marriage-void-despite-valid-certificate-if-saptapadi-not-performed-gujarat-high-court); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/18/patna-hc-voter-list-entry-alone-not-conclusive-proof-of-hindu-marriage/)
- Tamil Nadu Registration of Marriages Act, 2009 (TN Act 21 of 2009): compulsory registration of all marriages in Tamil Nadu regardless of religion, including marriages already registered under HMA/SMA; "priest" defined as any person who performs a marriage or is present at a marriage under s.7-A HMA; Tamil Nadu Marriages Registration Rules 2009 require a memorandum which the Registrar verifies. — [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f11969fca195c2c0cee14); [TN press release 30 Nov 2009](https://kygcms.tn.gov.in/cms_migrated/document/press_release/pr301109_e_905.pdf); [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/20505)
- Kerala Registration of Marriages (Common) Rules, 2008: Rule 9(1) memorandum in duplicate (Form I) with two sets of photos within 45 days of solemnisation; Rule 9(3) signed by both parties and two witnesses to the marriage, with proof of marriage — for a religious marriage "a copy of the certificate issued by the religious authority"; fee Rs 10; Rule 9(4) 45 days-1 year: Local Registrar may register after Rs 100 fine; Rule 10 after 1 year: with Registrar General's permission and Rs 250 fine, plus Form II declaration by a Gazetted Officer/MP/MLA/LSG member; Rule 11(1) certificate in Form IV within five working days (Rs 5). — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/the-kerala-registration-of-marriages-common-rules-2008/DFDB)
- Kerala HC (2025): the marriage registrar cannot decide validity of a marriage; disputes go to civil court; and Rule 9 does not compel both parties to be present before the local registrar. — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/kerala-high-court/2025ker40760-1581635); [BCAJ](https://bcajonline.org/journal/marriage-registration-presence-of-both-parties-to-marriage-before-local-registrar-not-necessary-hindu-marriage-act-section-8/)
- Kerala e-filing of marriage registration is through cr.lsgkerala.gov.in (general instructions and fee pages exist; site returned 503 on fetch). — [cr.lsgkerala.gov.in](https://cr.lsgkerala.gov.in/pages/efilegeneralinstrns.php)
- Karnataka Marriages (Registration and Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1976 (Karnataka Act 2 of 1984), commencement 1984; Kaveri 2.0 portal (kaveri.karnataka.gov.in) allows online marriage-certificate applications; third-party guides list Aadhaar, age proof, wedding invitation, and ID/address proof of 3 witnesses; fees reported inconsistently (Rs 10 application fee vs Rs 100-1,000). — [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f11549fca195c2c0ceb41); [Housing.com on Kaveri 2.0](https://housing.com/news/karnataka-launches-project-to-obtain-marriage-certificate-over-kaveri-2-0/amp); [BankBazaar](https://bankbazaar.com/govt-utility/marriage-certificate-in-karnataka.html)
- Tirumala: TTD certificate is ceremonial; register at Hindu Marriage Registrar, Room 233, SMC Guest House Complex, Tirumala with age/residence proof, wedding photograph, kalyana mandapam receipt, invitation and three witnesses' signatures. — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/news/planning-to-get-married-in-tirumala-heres-how-to-register-197575)
- Guruvayur: temple performs only the religious ceremony; legal registration at Guruvayur Municipality after verification with temple marriage certificate, Aadhaar, photos, DOB proof and two witnesses with ID; a marriage registration centre was opened near the temple (unofficial site). — [guruvayurtemple.org](https://guruvayurtemple.org/guruvayur-marriage-booking/); [guruvayurtemple.org registration centre](https://guruvayurtemple.org/?p=15253)
- Yadadri: hall manager issues only a receipt; registration at sub-registrar with receipt, invitation, photos/videos, Aadhaar. — [Gokshetra](https://gokshetra.com/yadagirigutta-temple-marriage-procedure-online-booking-hall-cost)
- Tamil Nadu temples: temple issues a certificate after the ceremony which is taken to the registrar for the government certificate (needed e.g. for visas). — [Hindu Blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2014/05/documents-and-rules-and-regulations-for.html?hl=en)
- NRI marriages: the Registration of Marriage of Non-Resident Indian Bill, 2019 (introduced Rajya Sabha 11 Feb 2019) would require every NRI marrying an Indian citizen to register within 30 days, with passport impounding and property attachment for proclaimed offenders; referred to the Standing Committee on External Affairs (report 13 March 2020); not enacted per PRS and other sources (one site wrongly claims implementation). — [PRS Bill Track](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-registration-of-marriage-of-non-resident-indian-bill-2019); [PRS Standing Committee summary](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/prs-products/prs-standing-committee-report-summary-3179); [Immihelp](https://immihelp.com/nri-marriage-registration-bill-what-you-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- The planner should collect at the ceremony: the temple's wedding certificate/receipt (religious-authority certificate for Kerala Rule 9(3); mandapam receipt for TTD/Telangana), the priest's name and signature (TN 2009 Act contemplates the "priest" as a party to the memorandum), photographs of the saptapadi/thali-tying, the invitation card, and ID/address proof of 2-3 witnesses (Kerala: 2; TTD/Karnataka guides: 3). Given the case law, photographic/video proof of saptapadi is valuable evidence of s.7 compliance.
- NRI couples face no distinct statutory deadline today, but foreign-use certificates typically need the state registration (plus apostille), so registration promptly (within Kerala's 45 days / TN's statutory window) remains the practical rule.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the Tamil Nadu 2009 Act's section texts (time limit for registration, fees, penalty) — casemine/indiacode/press-release pages returned 403/503; third-party guides commonly cite a 90-day window and late fees but these were not verified.
- Karnataka official document list/fee schedule on Kaveri 2.0 not verified from an official page.
- Whether any registrar formally "accepts" a specific temple certificate as sufficient proof is not documented officially; the Kerala rules are the clearest in requiring the religious authority's certificate.

## Key Question 6: Event-related permits around a temple town — processions, loudspeakers, drones, fire safety, food safety, pandals

### Takeaway
Loudspeakers require written permission under the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 and are barred 10 pm-6 am except inside closed halls (state governments may extend to midnight on up to 15 festival days a year); ASI event conditions independently cut off music at 10 pm. Drones fall under the Drone Rules 2021 (green/yellow/red zones on the Digital Sky platform), and ASI signage at Hampi bars drones outright. No reliable sources were found in this research on police intimation for baraat processions, fire-safety rules for homa inside halls, FSSAI requirements for wedding caterers, or municipal pandal permits.

### Cited Findings
- Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000, Rule 5: loudspeakers/public address systems shall not be used except after written permission from the authority; not at night (10 pm-6 am) except in closed premises (auditoria, conference rooms, community halls, banquet halls) or public emergency; State Government may permit use up to midnight on cultural/religious festive occasions not exceeding 15 days in a calendar year; loudspeaker noise at the boundary of a public place shall not exceed 10 dB(A) above ambient standards or 75 dB(A), whichever is lower. — [elaw.in text of Rules](https://elaw.in/noise/rules.html); [Nagaland IPR district notice](https://ipr.nagaland.gov.in/node/8960); [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f12609fca195c05ccf1ae)
- Karnataka HC (2021) questioned the basis on which indefinite loudspeaker permissions are issued under Rule 5(3). — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/karnataka-high-court-mosques-loudspeakers-permitted-in-which-law-noise-pollution-185675)
- ASI event conditions: functions must end by 10 pm with no music/loudspeakers after; temporary structures removed same day by 11 pm; conditions on parking, visitor numbers, noise and light. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/guidelines-in-place-relating-to-use-of-asi-sites-for-holding-cultural-events-govt/1476626)
- Drone Rules 2021: airspace divided into green, yellow and red zones; prior permission required for red and yellow zones; green zone needs none but the pilot must check the Digital Sky platform for temporary restrictions; States/UTs/law-enforcement may declare temporary red zones for up to 48 hours; remote pilot certificate required; flying in yellow/red zones without permission is a cognizable, non-compoundable offence. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/aviation/1108844/the-drone-rules-2021-giving-wings-to-the-drone-industry-in-india); [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/The-Drone-Rules-2021-Giving-wings-to-the-drone-industry-in-india); [Drishti airspace map](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/airspace-map-of-india/print_manually)
- Hampi: ASI boards state drone photography not permitted; special permission still required for movie shooting and drones over Hampi even after tourist camera charges were removed. — [Hospet Online](https://hospet.online/strict-regulations-on-photography-at-hampi/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/row-over-video-shoot-near-2068922)
- Hampi shoots require sign-off from HWHAMA, DC, SP and ASI (2020 report). — [Udayavani](https://udayavani.com/english-news/viral-video-probe-ordered-into-pre-wedding-photoshoot-at-hampi-heritage-site)

### Inferences
- For a baraat or reception near an ASI zone, the 10 pm loudspeaker cutoff is doubly enforced (Noise Rules + ASI conditions), so evening functions should be in closed halls; obtain written loudspeaker permission from the district authority named under the state's Noise Rules notification.
- Drone footage near a protected monument should be assumed prohibited unless the Digital Sky map shows green and ASI gives written consent; near Hampi assume no.
