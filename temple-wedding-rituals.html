Hindu temple wedding rituals, tradition by tradition

Every community has its own sequence, and the elders will notice if a step is missing. This is the order we plan to, tradition by tradition, with what each rite means.

The spine every tradition shares

However the names differ, a Vedic wedding turns on the same acts: the giving of the bride (Kanyadaana), the acceptance of her hand (Panigrahana), the tying of the mangalya or thali, the seven steps around the fire (Saptapadi), and the sighting of Arundhati, the star of fidelity. Under the Hindu Marriage Act the marriage is complete at the seventh step where saptapadi is part of the rite, which is why we never let a purohit compress it. Around that spine each community adds its own prelude and its own homecoming.

Kannada traditions

  1. Nischitartha (engagement): the families exchange tambula and fix the muhurta.
  2. Naandi: ancestors and the family deity are invoked at both homes; the kalasha is established.
  3. Kashi Yatre: the groom sets off "to Kashi" to become a renunciate and is talked back into marriage by the bride's father.
  4. Varapooja: the bride's family receives and honours the groom.
  5. Dhare Herdu (Kanyadaana): the bride's parents pour water over the joined hands, the act that gives the Kannada wedding its name.
  6. Mangalya Dharane: the thali is tied at the muhurta, with the drums at their loudest.
  7. Saptapadi and Laja Homa: seven steps and the offering of puffed rice into the fire by the bride's brother.
  8. Arundhati darshana, then Nagavalli (the playful post-wedding rites) and Grihapravesha, the bride's entry into her new home.

Madhwa families add their own Vedic emphasis and dietary strictness; Lingayat weddings follow the Veerashaiva rite with the jangama rather than a Vedic purohit. We brief accordingly.

Tamil Iyer and Iyengar

  1. Nischayathartham and Vratham: engagement, then the protective thread tied on bride and groom the day before.
  2. Kasi Yatrai and Oonjal: the groom's mock pilgrimage, then the couple on the swing while women sing and circle lamps.
  3. Kanyadaanam with the bride on her father's lap, then Mangalya Dharanam as the nadaswaram reaches its peak and the three knots are tied.
  4. Sapthapadi and Ammi mithithal (the bride steps on the grindstone), then Arundhati.
  5. Nalangu: the evening games.

Iyengar weddings add Pancharatra or Vaikhanasa agama elements and the Sri Vaishnava thiruman; the purohit must be from that parampara, which is why we match purohits by sampradaya, not by availability.

Telugu

  1. Nischitartham, then Pendlikoothuru (the turmeric bath and blessing of bride and groom at their homes).
  2. Kashi Yatra and Gauri pooja by the bride.
  3. Kanyadaanam; then Jeelakarra Bellam, the cumin-and-jaggery paste placed on each other's heads at the exact muhurta, which in Telugu tradition is the moment of marriage.
  4. Mangalya Dharanam with the thali of three knots, Talambralu (the showering of rice and pearls), then Saptapadi and Arundhati.
  5. Appagintalu: the bride is formally handed to the groom's family.

Malayali

The Kerala Hindu wedding is the shortest and the most temple-centred. At Guruvayur the entire rite is the Thalikettu, the tying of the thali before the deity, followed by Pudavakodukkal, the groom's gift of the wedding saree, with the couple circling the lamp or the mandapam. The feast, the sadya on banana leaf, follows at a hall. Nair, Ezhava and Nambudiri families differ in who ties the thali and in the role of the maternal uncle, and we follow the family's practice rather than a textbook.

North Indian Vedic

  1. Roka and Sagai, then Haldi and Mehndi at home.
  2. Baraat and Milni, then Jaimala, the exchange of garlands.
  3. Kanyadaan, Hastamilap, Gathbandhan and the Mangal Phere (four or seven circuits of the fire, with the Saat Vachan vows).
  4. Sindoor daan and mangalsutra, then Vidaai and Griha Pravesh.

At Triyuginarayan in Uttarakhand the pheras are taken around the Akhand Dhuni, the eternal fire said to have witnessed Shiva and Parvati's wedding, which is why northern families travel there.

How the sequence fits a temple

Most temples permit only the main rite inside: the thali at Guruvayur, a short ceremony in a sannathi at Madurai, a bare ritual at Hampi with no feast or photography. The rest of the sequence, from Naandi to Nalangu, happens at the hotel or a mantapa nearby, which is how a temple wedding becomes a two- or three-day event. We plan the full sequence first and then fit the temple's slot into it, never the other way round. See which temples allow what.

Questions

Our two families follow different traditions. Which sequence do you use?

Both. In the discussion rounds we sit with the elders of each family, list every rite and its order, and build a combined sequence in which nothing either family considers essential is dropped. The purohits are briefed with that document.

How long does the full ritual sequence take?

A Kannada or Tamil wedding with the full pre-wedding rites runs across two days; a Telugu wedding with Pendlikoothuru similarly; a Malayali temple wedding is minutes at the temple plus the sadya. We never ask a purohit to "finish by lunch".

Can the homa be performed inside the temple?

Rarely. Most temples restrict fire inside the complex (Bhoga Nandeeshwara permits ghee lamps only). The homa happens at the hall or mantapa where the pre-wedding rituals are held, with a proper homa kunda and fire safety.
